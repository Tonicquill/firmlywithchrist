Iran: Nine Years for Owning a Bible. The Word of God Is Not Chained.

Ghazal Marzban, an Iranian Christian convert, was sentenced to nine years and eight months for possessing a Bible. The Revolutionary Court called it propaganda against the state. The Bible calls it obedience.

Persecution 2 Timothy 2:9 Hebrews 13:3 Revelation 2:10
Read below ↓

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

On Monday, Revolutionary Court judge Iman Afshari — previously sanctioned by the European Union for overseeing cases against political prisoners and Christians — sentenced Ghazal Marzban to nine years and eight months in prison. Her crime was possessing a Bible and Christian literature in her home in Tehran. The court called it "propaganda against the state" and "gathering and collusion against national security." Marzban called it faith. She had already spent two months in prison in 2024 for chanting slogans against the regime. She was rearrested in January, held for a month without outside contact, and pressured during interrogations to claim her Bible was for evangelism. She refused. The Bible was for her. The state decided that made her dangerous.[1]

Paul wrote from a Roman prison: "for which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained" (2 Timothy 2:9). The Iranian Revolutionary Guard can confiscate a Bible. It cannot confiscate the Word. It can sentence a woman to nine years for owning Christian literature. It cannot sentence the literature to silence. The same Spirit who breathed Scripture into existence breathes faith into the prisoner who reads it by flashlight in a Ministry of Intelligence detention centre. The state believes it is chaining a criminal. It is only chaining itself to the verdict it will face on the last day.

for which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained.

2 Timothy 2:9

The writer to the Hebrews commanded: "Remember the prisoners as if chained with them — those who are mistreated — since you yourselves are in the body also" (Hebrews 13:3). Ghazal Marzban is not a statistic. She is a sister. She converted to Catholicism seven years ago, studied Islamic law, was prevented from sitting her bar exams, and encouraged to leave the country. She stayed. Her husband, also a convert, lives with Parkinson's disease and now faces the prospect of managing his illness alone while his wife serves a sentence that amounts to a life sentence for both of them. Mansour Borji of Article18 said it plainly: "Due to her husband's condition, this is in reality a sentence for both of them." To remember the prisoner is to recognize that the church is one body, that a cell in Tehran is a cell in the body of Christ, and that every Christian who reads their Bible in safety today owes something to the Christian who reads theirs in chains.

Jesus spoke to the church in Smyrna: "Do not fear any of those things which you are about to suffer. Indeed, the devil is about to throw some of you into prison, that you may be tested, and you will have tribulation ten days. Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life" (Revelation 2:10). Nine years and eight months is longer than ten days. But the promise does not expire. The crown of life is not a metaphor for comfort. It is a guarantee that the prisoner who dies in chains will rise in glory. The Iranian regime has sentenced Ghazal Marzban for the crime of possessing God's Word. What it has actually done is guarantee her possession of the crown of life. The judge who signed the sentence believes he is administering justice. He is only writing his own indictment.

The world will forget Ghazal Marzban by next month. The European Union sanctioned Judge Afshari, but sanctions do not open prison doors. The international community will issue statements, and the church in the West will scroll past the headline. But the Word of God is not chained. The same Bible that Marzban refused to renounce will outlast every Revolutionary Court, every anti-conversion law, every regime that has tried to write itself into God's office. The state took her Bible. It did not take her faith. It gave her a cell. It did not give her a verdict. That belongs to the righteous Judge who will by no means clear the guilty — and who reserves a crown of life for every saint who was faithful until death, or until the door finally opens, whichever comes first.

Nine years and eight months for owning a Bible. The Iranian Revolutionary Court believes it has chained Ghazal Marzban. It has not. The word of God is not chained. The prisoner is not abandoned. And the crown of life is reserved for every saint who refuses to renounce the Book that sentenced them.
