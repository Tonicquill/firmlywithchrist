Reformed theology for a shaken world — Scripture-grounded. Confessionally rooted.

May 16, 2026

Thirty-six nations signed a treaty to prosecute Putin. God signed none. He didn't need to.

Amos 1 says God judges nations for war crimes without waiting for a signed treaty. Every human court is provisional. Every judge will be judged.

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

Thirty-six nations signed a treaty this week to create a special tribunal at The Hague — a court purpose-built to prosecute Vladimir Putin and his inner circle for the crime of aggression against Ukraine. The signatories are thirty-four European states plus Australia and Costa Rica. The tribunal targets the "troika": president, prime minister, foreign minister. It can impose life imprisonment, confiscate property, levy fines. The European Union committed ten million euros. Four EU states refused to sign — Bulgaria, Hungary, Malta, Slovakia. The guns have not stopped. The legal machinery is starting. Paul wrote about this moment two thousand years before it arrived: "The magistrate does not bear the sword in vain."[1][2]

Romans 13 does not promise that every magistrate who bears the sword bears it justly. It declares that every magistrate who bears the sword bears it under God. The text is precise: "He is God's minister to you for good. But if you do evil, be afraid; for he does not bear the sword in vain; for he is God's minister, an avenger to execute wrath on him who practices evil" (Romans 13:4). The sword is not ornamental. It is the state's God-given authority to punish evil — not to manage it, not to negotiate with it, not to issue a statement about it. The magistrate is an avenger. The Greek word is ekdikos — the one who executes justice on behalf of another. Paul's argument is that civil authority exists precisely because evil exists. A state that cannot punish wrongdoing is not a state. It is a theater production.

The Reformed tradition takes Romans 13 seriously enough to reject two errors at once. Error one: the state is God's mouthpiece and can do no wrong. That is the error of absolute monarchy, and Calvin rejected it in the Institutes (4.20.24–29) by affirming that lesser magistrates may resist tyranny. Error two: the state is an autonomous zone where God's moral law does not apply. That is the error of modern realpolitik, and every confession worth its salt rejects it. The Westminster Confession (23.1) teaches that God ordains civil magistrates "for the defense and encouragement of them that are good, and for the punishment of evil doers." Not punishment of some evil doers. Not punishment of evil doers who lack diplomatic immunity. Punishment of evil doers. Period. The confession has no footnote that reads "unless the evil doer runs a nuclear state."

For the kingdom of this world will become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ.

Revelation 11:15

But Romans 13 is not the only text that speaks to the tribunal room in The Hague. Amos 1 is — and Amos is harder to sit with. God's judgment oracles against the nations in Amos 1:3–2:3 are the most politically incorrect texts in the Bible for a moment like this. God does not judge only Israel. He judges Damascus. He judges Gaza. He judges Tyre. He judges Edom, Ammon, and Moab. Each indictment follows a formula: "For three transgressions of [nation], and for four, I will not turn away its punishment." And the charge against Ammon is specific: "They ripped open the women with child in Gilead, that they might enlarge their territory" (Amos 1:13). This is war crime language — mass atrocity committed for territorial expansion. Amos 1:13 is God indicting a foreign ruler for what modern law would call a crime of aggression. God did not wait for a signed treaty. He did not wait for the Council of Europe. He indicted Ammon through a Judean shepherd three thousand years before The Hague existed.

The prophet's point is not that one nation's foreign policy is bad. The point is that God holds rulers accountable for atrocities committed against other nations. The Ammonites did not attack Israel. They attacked Gilead. God still judged them. The sovereignty of God over the nations is not a theological abstraction. It means that every war, every invasion, every bomb that hits a maternity ward falls under the jurisdiction of the Judge of all the earth. The International Criminal Court is provisional. The throne of God is not. The tribunal in The Hague can prosecute the crime of aggression. Only God can judge the heart that ordered it. Both truths are biblical. Neither cancels the other.

Genesis 9:6 grounds criminal accountability in the image of God: "Whoever sheds man's blood, by man his blood shall be shed; for in the image of God He made man" (Genesis 9:6). God does not take the throne and judge every crime directly. He delegates justice to human institutions. The shedding of blood is punished by human courts "by man" — the language is institutional, not vigilante. This is the foundation of all criminal law. And the reason given is theological: man bears God's image. The victim of a war crime is not a number. The victim bears the likeness of the Creator. The tribunal in The Hague is not an act of political theater. It is an imperfect human echo of a perfect divine mandate: image-bearers are not disposable.

Proverbs 17:15 provides the warning that makes this whole framework dangerous to apply selectively: "He who justifies the wicked, and he who condemns the just, both of them alike are an abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 17:15). Any international tribunal is subject to this text. It must operate with actual justice — actual evidence, actual due process, actual law. A show trial is just as abominable as no trial. The church should affirm the legitimacy of the tribunal's stated purpose. It should not baptize its every decision. Justice that is blind is biblical. Justice that is weaponized is "an abomination to the LORD." The sword Paul describes in Romans 13 is wielded against actual evil, not political inconvenience. That is the standard. No human court fully meets it. But the standard remains.

There is a limit to every human tribunal, and the signatories in The Hague would do well to remember it. Revelation 11:15 declares the terminus of all earthly justice: "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ" (Revelation 11:15). Every provisional tribunal is provisional. Every signed treaty expires. Every judge will be judged. The day is coming when the ICC, the new special tribunal, Putin, the troika, every signatory and every non-signatory, every Ukrainian civilian whose name was never recorded and every Russian conscript who never wanted to be there — all of them will stand before a Judge who does not need a treaty to exercise jurisdiction. And His court does not permit trials in absentia. Every defendant will be present. Every verdict will be final. Every appeal will be exhausted. No diplomatic immunity before the throne of Christ.

Putin is immune from trials in absentia while in office. That is a legal limit, not a theological one. His immunity expires — if not before a court in The Hague, then before the Judge who needs no court. That is the hope and the warning. It is hope for victims who have waited too long for justice to arrive in an armored vehicle with diplomatic plates. It is warning for rulers who mistake the patience of God for the absence of God. 2 Timothy 2:19 is the anchor: "The Lord knows those who are His" and "Let everyone who names the name of Christ depart from iniquity." Both seals hold. Both statements are true at the same time. God knows His own. And everyone who names His name must depart from evil. The tribunal in The Hague can put a name on a warrant. Only Christ can write a name in the book of life.

Thirty-six nations signed a treaty. God signed none. He did not need to. The Judge of all the earth does not await permission from the Council of Europe. Every provisional tribunal expires. Every defendant will appear. Every gavel will fall. And the court that matters most does not permit diplomatic immunity.

Family

Sixty years of secular research landed on a formula: warmth plus boundaries equals flourishing kids. Proverbs wrote it down 3,000 years ago. Ephesians added what the research cannot: children are image-bearers AND sinners, and the only power that sustains loving authority is grace.
Ecclesiology

The Vatican issued a final warning to SSPX: consecrate four new bishops without papal consent and incur automatic excommunication. The SSPX plans to proceed. Both sides assume someone other than Christ is Head of the church. The Reformation called this wrong in 1521. It's still wrong.
Persecution

They walked into a conflict zone to make peace. They were killed doing it.

Three Kuki Baptist church leaders were killed in Manipur while carrying a mandate for reconciliation between ethnic rivals. Jesus said peacemakers are called sons of God. The world kills them. God calls them family.
Ecclesiology

Egypt legalized 191 churches. Christ authorized one.

Christ authorized the church at Calvary, commissioned it at the Great Commission, and will present it to Himself in glory. No government participates in any of those acts.
