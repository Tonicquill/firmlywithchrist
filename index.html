China Arrests Six for Teaching Children the Gospel. The State's Oldest Fear.

Six house-church Christians in Guizhou Province were arrested for organizing a children's Sunday school. The charge was not heresy. It was teaching children.

Persecution Deuteronomy 6:6-7 Acts 5:29 Matthew 19:14
Read below ↓

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

Six house-church Christians in Kaili, Guizhou Province, were arrested this week for the crime of teaching children the Bible. The state called it "fraud" and "organizing activities involving minors that undermine public order." The church calls it obedience. The accusation has changed across centuries, but the conflict is the same: the state wants jurisdiction over minds that belong to God.[1]

The Reformed tradition does not treat the instruction of children as a discretionary program. It treats it as a binding mandate from the King of Kings. Deuteronomy 6:6-7 places the duty squarely on the shoulders of parents and the household of faith: teach them diligently. Not when the state approves. Not when the curriculum clears review. Diligently. Always. In every posture of life. To arrest a Christian for teaching children the gospel is not a bureaucratic correction. It is an act of war against a divine command.

And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children.

Deuteronomy 6:6-7

The six detainees — Wei Yongqiang, He Jinbao, Quan Xiaolong, Long Jian, Cheng Yongbing, and Zhou Guixia — were not charged with violence or fraud in any ordinary sense. They were charged with the crime of letting children hear the gospel. The specific charge, "organizing minors to engage in activities undermining public order," has historically been reserved for inciting minors to criminal conduct. Applying it to Sunday school is a dangerous expansion — one that potentially criminalizes every parent who prays with a child or reads Scripture at bedtime. Acts 5:29 was written for exactly this collision. When a ruler claims authority over the spiritual formation of the next generation, the church's answer has always been the same: we must obey God rather than men.

Jesus said, "Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid them; for of such is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 19:14). The state that forbids children from coming to Christ is not exercising neutral governance. It is claiming sovereignty over souls. The Westminster Confession of Faith establishes the absolute sufficiency of Scripture for all that concerns faith and life — including the urgent mandate to proclaim the gospel to every generation. No provincial procuratorate has the authority to amend that mandate.

This is not a distant problem. The Chinese house church has endured systematic pressure for decades, from the Three-Self Patriotic Movement's control to revised public security laws that place "illegal religious activities" on the same plane as cult involvement. But to target children is to target the church's future. The arc is not hidden. Pharaoh ordered the male infants killed. The Sanhedrin sought to silence the apostles. Every regime that has tried to write itself into God's office has eventually been weighed on the scales and found deficient. The church in Kaili is not the first to face this choice, and it will not be the first to refuse.

The Chinese state arrested six Christians for teaching children the gospel. The same state that cannot save a soul wants to supervise every soul that hears of salvation. The church has heard this demand before. It has never obeyed it.
