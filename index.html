Reformed theology for a shaken world — Scripture-grounded. Confessionally rooted.

May 21, 2026

A priest beaten in Dhaka. A convert tied up by a mob of five hundred. This is May 2026.

A priest beaten and robbed in Dhaka. A convert tied up by a mob of five hundred. A missionary of twenty-two years driven from the slum. Bangladesh, May 2026.

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

A priest was beaten and robbed at St. Eugene de Mazenod Church in Dhaka. A mob of five to seven hundred people attacked a Christian convert named Atahar at a market, tied him up, publicly humiliated him, and destroyed his tea stall. An evangelist named Mizan was gathered against by a local Islamic teacher and told to renounce his faith or leave his village. A bull was stolen from St. Francis GBR Catholic Mission in Shaistaganj on May 1, slaughtered behind the mission, and carted away for meat. The mission runs a center for children with disabilities. Brother Lucio Beninati, a seventy-year-old Italian missionary who has worked in Dhaka's slums for twenty-two years, has been detained and interrogated by police multiple times on false charges of proselytizing and child trafficking. He can no longer live in the communities he served. These are not ancient stories. They are May 2026. Bangladesh.[1]

Jesus sent His disciples out with a warning, not a pep talk. "Behold, I send you out as sheep in the midst of wolves. Therefore be wise as serpents and harmless as doves. But beware of men, for they will deliver you up to councils and scourge you in their synagogues. You will be brought before governors and kings for My sake, as a testimony to them and to the Gentiles" (Matthew 10:16-18). The verb is future tense, but the force is present reality. Jesus did not say "they might." He said "they will." The beating of a priest in Dhaka is not a surprise to the One who predicted it. The mob that tied up Atahar in the market is not doing something Jesus failed to anticipate. The teacher who gathered a group to pressure Mizan is not inventing a new form of hostility. He is fulfilling an old one. The only question is whether the church will receive the persecution as testimony — or as defeat.

The New Testament does not treat persecution as interruption. It treats it as appointment. "All who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution" (2 Timothy 3:12). The word will is not conditional on geography or politics. It is conditional on godliness. The Bangladeshi Christian who was beaten in his own church, the convert who was tied up in his own market, the missionary who was driven from his own slum — they are not experiencing an administrative anomaly. They are experiencing the pattern of the first generation of church history. The world did not stop hating the gospel when Rome legalized Christianity. It changed tactics. Mobs replaced lions. False charges replaced crucifixion. But the hostility is the same, and the reason is the same: "Everyone practicing evil hates the light and does not come to the light, lest his deeds should be exposed" (John 3:20). The mob in Atahar's market hated the light. So they broke the lamp.

Blessed are you when they revile and persecute you, and say all kinds of evil against you falsely for My sake. Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven, for so they persecuted the prophets who were before you.

Matthew 5:11-12

But Jesus did not only warn. He blessed. "Blessed are you when they revile and persecute you... Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven" (Matthew 5:11-12). The blessing is not for the persecution itself. The blessing is for what the persecution proves: you are on the same side as the prophets. The priest in Dhaka is in the line of Jeremiah, who was beaten and put in stocks. Atahar is in the line of the apostles, who were dragged before councils. Mizan is in the line of Stephen, who was stoned for refusing to renounce what he had seen. The reward is not a compensation for pain. It is a confirmation of identity. Great is your reward — not because the suffering is great, but because the One who sees it is great.

The robbery adds another dimension. Hebrews 10:34 describes believers who "joyfully accepted the plundering of your goods, knowing that you have a better and an enduring possession for yourselves in heaven." Joyfully accepted the plundering. The priest who was robbed of his valuables at St. Eugene de Mazenod Church, the mission whose bull was stolen and slaughtered — they are not mere victims of crime. They are participants in a biblical pattern. The goods are plundered. The possession remains. The tea stall is destroyed. The reward is not. Brother Beninati lost his slum community, his freedom of movement, his reputation. But he did not lose what he was bought for. "You were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's" (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). The price was not paid for comfort. It was paid for glory.

The watching world asks: where is God when His people are beaten and robbed and driven from their homes? The answer is in the sparrow. "Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Do not fear therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows" (Matthew 10:29-31). The sparrows in Bangladesh are cheap. The Christians are not. Every blow is numbered. Every robbery is witnessed. Every expulsion is recorded by the One who numbers hairs. The Father who saw the sparrow fall saw the priest beaten. The Father who numbered the hairs numbered the mob. Nothing — not a police interrogation, not a false charge, not a slaughtered bull, not five hundred people in a market — happens apart from His will. That is not a claim that the violence is good. It is a claim that God is still God when the violence happens.

The final word is security, not safety. "For I am persuaded that neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities nor powers, nor things present nor things to come, nor height nor depth, nor any other created thing, shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 8:38-39). The mob can separate Atahar from his tea stall. It cannot separate him from Christ. The police can separate Brother Beninati from the slum. They cannot separate him from the love of God. The teacher can pressure Mizan to renounce his faith. He cannot make the renunciation true. The foundation holds its seal: "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19). The teacher does not know those who are His. The mob does not know. The government that changed in February 2026 does not know. But the Lord knows. And His knowing is their keeping.

A priest beaten in Dhaka. A convert tied up in a market. A missionary driven from the slum. The mob thought it was breaking the church. It was proving the promise: "Blessed are you when they persecute you." The blessing is not compensation. It is confirmation. You are on the right side.

Salvation

Union with Christ is not a metaphor. It is the channel through which every blessing flows.

Ask the average Christian how they are saved, and you will hear a list. Ask what connects those blessings to their actual life, and the answer gets quieter.
Covenant

The Bible is not sixty-six books that happen to share a binding.

Most readers treat the Bible like an anthology. The Bible itself has never read it that way.
Justice

Thirty-six nations signed a treaty to prosecute Putin. God signed none. He didn't need to.

Thirty-six nations created a special tribunal at The Hague to prosecute Vladimir Putin for the crime of aggression against Ukraine. Romans 13 says the magistrate bears the sword as God's minister. Amos 1 says God judges nations for war crimes without waiting for a signed treaty. Every human court is provisional. Every judge will be judged.
Family

Some parents break their children with harshness. Some break them with neglect. The Bible forbids both.

Sixty years of secular research landed on a formula: warmth plus boundaries equals flourishing kids. Proverbs wrote it down 3,000 years ago. Ephesians added what the research cannot: children are image-bearers AND sinners, and the only power that sustains loving authority is grace.
Ecclesiology

Rome threatened excommunication. The SSPX shrugged. Neither asked the right question.

The Vatican issued a final warning to SSPX: consecrate four new bishops without papal consent and incur automatic excommunication. The SSPX plans to proceed. Both sides assume someone other than Christ is Head of the church. The Reformation called this wrong in 1521. It's still wrong.

Three Kuki Baptist church leaders were killed in Manipur while carrying a mandate for reconciliation between ethnic rivals. Jesus said peacemakers are called sons of God. The world kills them. God calls them family.

A few people with full-time jobs and full lives who sat under sound preaching and wanted to share it with friends — without sending ninety-minute sermons or walls of text no one would read. Doctrine meant to be thought with, not just recited.

What it isn't

Not a devotional. Not a church bulletin. Not a news aggregator. We're not pastors, theologians, or influencers. We don't chase trends or write for engagement. We write for readers who don't yet share the frame, alongside those who do.

How it's checked

Every piece is sourced against Scripture and primary texts, read as a secularist would read it, and checked against the experience of someone who left an abusive church. If it fails any gate, it goes back to draft. Read how we verify.
