Chhattisgarh: 180 Families Denied Water for Being Christian. Pharaoh Still Lives.

In Chhattisgarh, India, 180 Christian families across 32 villages have been denied access to communal water, government work, and firewood for three weeks. The goal is Ghar Wapsi — re-conversion to Hinduism. The tactic is older than the Hindu nationalist movement. It is exactly what Pharaoh did.

Persecution Exodus 5:17-18 Matthew 6:31-33 Revelation 7:16-17
Read below ↓

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

For three weeks, 180 Christian families across thirty-two villages in Kanker district, Chhattisgarh, have been cut off from the sources of life itself. Their neighbors have barred them from communal rivers, ponds, and hand pumps. Their employers have blocked them from MGNREGA work programs. Their collectors have refused them Tendu leaves — the seasonal leaf used for rolling cigarettes, a crucial source of income for Adivasi families in the region. Even firewood has been denied. The message is not subtle. Convert back to Hinduism, or thirst. Convert back, or starve. The campaign is called Ghar Wapsi — 'homecoming' — and it is being enforced not by law but by social boycott, village by village, family by family. The Progressive Christian Alliance and Jila Masih Astha Samaj have filed complaints with the district administration. No action has been taken. International Christian Concern reports that this is not the first time Kanker district Christians have been targeted. Earlier this year, Adivasi believers were barred from burying their dead in village cemeteries. Pastors were banned from entering villages. Now the water has been turned off. The Egyptians did the same to the Israelites. They just used different plumbing.[1]

Pharaoh's foremen confronted Moses with exactly the logic now being used in Kanker district: "You are idle! Idle! Therefore you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.' Therefore go now and work; for no straw shall be given you, yet you shall deliver the quota of bricks" (Exodus 5:17-18). Pharaoh did not oppose the Israelites because they were dangerous. He opposed them because they worshipped a God he did not control. His response was economic strangulation — increase the workload, remove the materials, make survival itself the price of fidelity. The village councils of Kanker have learned the same lesson. They cannot force the Christians to renounce Christ directly. So they remove the water. They remove the work. They remove the firewood. They make daily life bitter with hard bondage, hoping that faith will break before the body does. It will not.

You are idle! Idle! Therefore you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.' Therefore go now and work; for no straw shall be given you, yet you shall deliver the quota of bricks.

Exodus 5:17-18

Jesus told His disciples what to do when the world withholds the basics: "Therefore do not worry, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?'... For your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things. But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you" (Matthew 6:31-33). The Christians of Kanker district are not worrying about whether they will eat. They are worrying about whether they will deny the One who fed them. And that is precisely the worry Jesus addresses. The heavenly Father knows they need water. He knows they need work. He knows they need firewood and Tendu leaves and dignity. He also knows that the only thing they truly need is Him. The village council can turn off the hand pump. It cannot turn off the fountain of living water. The MGNREGA supervisor can withhold the work card. He cannot withhold the labor of Christ on their behalf. The Adivasi Christian who seeks first the kingdom of God in a dry village is not a fool. He is a heir.

John saw the final reckoning: "They shall neither hunger anymore nor thirst anymore; the sun shall not strike them, nor any heat; for the Lamb who is in the midst of the throne will shepherd them and lead them to living fountains of waters. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (Revelation 7:16-17). This is not a metaphor for spiritual comfort. It is a literal promise to literal people who literally thirsted. The 180 families of Kanker district will not thirst forever. The Lamb who sits on the throne is not indifferent to their dehydration. He is not unmoved by their empty cooking pots. He is shepherding them now — through the dry season, through the boycotted employment, through the village meetings where they are called idle for wanting to worship. And He will lead them to living fountains of waters. The village council thinks it is writing the ending. It is only writing the footnote.

The district administration has the complaints. The newspapers do not have the story. The international community will not have the outrage. But the church has the promise. The church remembers Joseph in prison, Daniel in the lions' den, Paul in chains writing that his imprisonment advanced the gospel. The church remembers that Pharaoh's bricks did not outlast the Exodus, that Nebuchadnezzar's furnace did not outlast the fourth man, that Herod's sword did not outlast the church. The 180 families of Kanker district are not victims of a social boycott. They are witnesses to the same sovereignty that turned water into blood in Egypt and living water in Samaria. The village council believes it is administering Ghar Wapsi — a homecoming to Hinduism. It is actually administering a homily. The whole world is watching, and the Judge of all the earth is taking notes.

180 Christian families across 32 villages in Chhattisgarh have been denied water, work, and firewood for three weeks to force them into Ghar Wapsi re-conversion. Pharaoh used the same tactic — remove the straw, increase the bricks, make faith expensive. The village councils think they are administering a homecoming. They are only proving that the God who brought water from the rock still shepherds His people through the desert.
