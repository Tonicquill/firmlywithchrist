Nigeria: Fulani Militia Kill Four Christians in Benue State. The Hairs on Their Heads Were Numbered.

Fulani militia killed four Christians across three counties in Benue State, Nigeria. AK-47s, machetes, and chants of Allahu Akbar. The authorities know where the camps are. They do nothing. God numbers the hairs on the heads of the slain.

Persecution Matthew 10:29-31 Matthew 23:23 Luke 18:7-8

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

On the evening of May 26, Muslim Fulani militia attacked three locations across Benue State, central Nigeria, and killed four Christians. In Ukpamaju village, Otukpo County, farmer Friday Omoha and tractor operator Francis Liambe were gunned down by roughly fifteen armed men chanting Allahu Akbar and wielding AK-47 rifles. Hours later, on the Otukpo–Enugu highway in Ohimini County, militants attacked a commercial bus and killed a security operative who tried to protect the Christian passengers. In Ojantele community, Apa County, twenty-five-year-old Ngbede Ocholongwa was killed. Otukpo County Chairman Maxwell Ogiri confirmed the incidents. Security analyst Pablo William called it a “sustained pattern of organized rural Islamic terror targeting vulnerable Christian farming communities.” Survivor Stephen Omoha hid in nearby bushes while the gunfire rang out and the militants spoke Fulfulde and Hausa. Activist Meddy Olotu said communities are living in constant fear. Resident John Ehoda said people believe the authorities know where the militia camps are but do nothing to dismantle them. The Nigerian federal government has already been accused by the U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom of “tolerating” religious violence and failing to prosecute killers. Over 1.3 million people have been displaced in the Middle Belt. Four Christians dead in one evening. And the authorities who should protect them have “neglected the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faith.”[1]

Jesus warned His disciples: "'Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Do not fear therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows'" (Matthew 10:29-31). The Fulani militant with the AK-47 does not know that Friday Omoha's hairs were numbered. He does not know that Francis Liambe was of more value than many sparrows. He does not know that Ngbede Ocholongwa fell to the ground apart from his Father's will — which means the Father saw it, the Father allowed it, and the Father has already numbered the days of the militant who pulled the trigger. The sparrow that falls is not an accident of rural violence. It is an appointment with the One who counts the hairs on the heads of the beheaded.

Matthew 10:29-31

Jesus condemned the religious authorities of His day: "'Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have neglected the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faith. These you ought to have done, without leaving the others undone'" (Matthew 23:23). The Nigerian federal government has not neglected mint and cummin. It has neglected justice. It has neglected mercy. It has neglected faith. The U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom has documented that the Nigerian government “tolerates” religious violence and fails to prosecute killers. John Ehoda, a resident of the attacked region, said what everyone knows: the authorities are aware of the militia camps but little has been done to dismantle them. When a state knows where the killers are and does nothing, it has not merely failed its citizens. It has become a participant in their deaths. The weightier matters of the law are not optional extras for a functioning government. They are the whole reason the sword exists.

Jesus told a parable about persistent prayer: "'And shall God not avenge His own elect who cry out day and night to Him, though He bears long with them? I tell you that He will avenge them speedily'" (Luke 18:7-8). The blood of Friday Omoha cries out. The blood of Francis Liambe cries out. The blood of Ngbede Ocholongwa cries out. The blood of the unnamed security operative who died defending a bus full of Christians cries out. And the Nigerian authorities who do nothing are not silencing those cries. They are only adding their own names to the indictment. God bears long with them. He is not slack. He is gathering evidence. The day is coming when the Judge of all the earth will weigh the Nigerian government on the scales and find it deficient — not because it lacked resources, but because it lacked the will to protect the sparrows whose hairs were already numbered by a higher Power.

The world will not remember the names of the four Christians killed in Benue State on May 26. The UN will not issue a statement. The international press will not send reporters. The Nigerian military will not launch an operation. And the church in Ukpamaju village will still meet on Sunday — diminished, displaced, but defiant. Because the church does not fear those who kill the body. The church fears the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell — and the church belongs to Him. The Christians of Benue State are not victims of farmer-herder clashes. They are witnesses to the sovereignty of God, the same God who numbers the hairs on the heads of the gunned-down, who bottles the tears of the widowed, and who promises that not one sparrow falls apart from His will. The militant who chants Allahu Akbar believes he is writing history. He is only writing his own indictment. The God who sees every sparrow will by no means clear the guilty. He will repay. And He will do it speedily.
