DRC: ADF Jihadis Massacre Christians Across Eastern Congo. The Gates of Hell Do Not Prevail.

In April and May 2026, ADF militants carried out a wave of massacres across eastern DRC, killing more than a hundred thirty civilians in Mambasa territory alone. The church does not die by the sword. It dies by compromise — and that sword has not touched it yet.

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

In April and May 2026, Allied Democratic Forces jihadis carried out a wave of massacres across Ituri and North Kivu provinces. On April 1, at least forty-three civilians were slaughtered in Bafwakoa. On May 7, twenty-four more died in Katerrain and Mangambo. By mid-May, local civil society groups reported more than one hundred thirty dead in Mambasa territory alone — in a region where churches and missionary schools are especially targeted.[1] The United States government formally condemned the ADF for "targeted violence against Christian communities."[2] The church does not die by the sword. It dies by compromise — and that sword has not touched it yet.

Jesus said, "And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell." (Matthew 10:28) The ADF militant with a machete can end a Christian's life in eastern Congo. He cannot end their life in Christ. He can burn a church building. He cannot burn the church. He can drive believers from their villages. He cannot drive them from the hand of the Father. The fear of the Lord is the antidote to the fear of men, and the church in Ituri has learned this calculus in blood.

And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell.

Matthew 10:28

Jesus promised Peter, "On this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it" (Matthew 16:18). The gates of hell are not a metaphor in eastern Congo. They are machetes, torched homes, and the Samboko River valley where militants now control villages. But the promise is not that the church will avoid the gates. The promise is that the gates will not prevail. The church in Oicha still meets — forty-two believers where there used to be a hundred thirty-six. It is diminished. It is not destroyed. And the gates of hell have no key that fits the lock Christ placed on His people's redemption.

Paul wrote to the Thessalonians: "But we do not want you to be uninformed, brothers, about those who are asleep, that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope. For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep." (1 Thessalonians 4:13-14) The families in Mambasa territory bury their dead with machete wounds and burn marks. They grieve. But they do not grieve as the world grieves. The ADF has taken husbands and wives, fathers and mothers. It has not taken the resurrection. The same Jesus who died and rose again will bring with Him every Christian who fell asleep in a burning hut or a forest ambush. That is not a comfort for the comfortable. It is a verdict on every machete.

The world will forget these villages by next month. The UN will issue another statement. The Congolese army will promise another operation. And the church will still meet on Sunday, diminished but defiant, because the gates of hell do not prevail. The ADF can kill the body. It cannot kill the soul. It can silence a congregation. It cannot silence the King. The church in eastern DRC is not a victim of circumstance. It is a witness to the sovereignty of God — the same God who numbers the hairs on the heads of the slaughtered, who bottles the tears of the widowed, and who will ride out of heaven on the Day of the Lord with a rod of iron and a robe dipped in blood. The militants believe they are writing the end of the story. They are only writing the footnotes.

More than a hundred thirty dead in Mambasa territory. Churches burned. Christians targeted. The ADF believes it can kill the church with a machete. It cannot. The church does not die by the sword. It dies by compromise — and that sword has not touched it yet.

A few people with full-time jobs and full lives who sat under sound preaching and wanted to share it with friends — without sending ninety-minute sermons or walls of text no one would read. Doctrine meant to be thought with, not just recited.

What it isn't

Not a devotional. Not a church bulletin. Not a news aggregator. We're not pastors, theologians, or influencers. We don't chase trends or write for engagement. We write for readers who don't yet share the frame, alongside those who do.

How it's checked

Every piece is sourced against Scripture and primary texts, read as a secularist would read it, and checked against the experience of someone who left an abusive church. If it fails any gate, it goes back to draft. Read how we verify.
