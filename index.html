Reformed theology for a shaken world — Scripture-grounded. Confessionally rooted.

May 20, 2026

Eleven pastors killed in Colombia. The Lord knows those who are His.

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16

Eleven religious leaders in Colombia have been killed, disappeared, or kidnapped since December 2024. Pastor Marlon Lora was shot dead with his wife and two adult children while eating lunch after Sunday services in the Cesar Department. Pastor Darío Iván García was gunned down by masked hitmen in Magdalena after a prayer service. Pastor José Otoniel Ortega was shot on New Year's Eve with his family beside him. Seven Protestant leaders from Guaviare were summoned by a guerrilla group in April 2025; their bodies were found in a mass grave three months later. The Colombian government removed religious leaders from its official protection list in 2023. Christian Solidarity Worldwide launched a global campaign this month to reverse that decision before the May 31 presidential election. The petition will be presented to the winner. Some of the pastors will not be there to see it.[1]

Stephen's sermon in Acts 7 ends with a question, not a plea. "Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute? And they killed those who foretold the coming of the Just One, of whom you now have become the betrayers and murderers" (Acts 7:52). The question is rhetorical because the answer is universal: all of them. From Abel to Zechariah, from Jeremiah to John the Baptist, the pattern is uninterrupted. The world does not reject religious leaders because they fail. It rejects them because they speak. Jesus said the world hated Him before it hated His followers, and it will hate them for the same reason: "Whoever kills you will think that he offers God service" (John 16:2). The guerrilla group that summoned seven pastors to a mass grave thought they were solving a problem. They were proving a prophecy.

But the Bible does not leave the persecuted with a history lesson. It gives them a promise. "Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Do not fear therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows" (Matthew 10:29-31). The sparrows are cheap. The pastors are not. Every bullet, every burial, every moment of terror is counted by the One who numbers hairs. The government that removed pastors from the protection list in 2023 did not remove them from the Father's sight. The guerrilla group that summoned seven men to a grave did not summon them out of God's governance. Nothing — not a policy change, not a masked hitman, not a mass grave in the jungle — happens apart from the Father's will. That is not a claim that the violence is good. It is a claim that God is still God when the violence happens.

For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

Philippians 1:21

Paul wrote to Philippi from prison, awaiting a trial that might end in execution. His verdict was already rendered: "To live is Christ, and to die is gain" (Philippians 1:21). The pastor who is shot after a prayer service does not lose. He trades a pulpit for a throne. The church leader who is dumped in a mass grave does not disappear. He is received up in glory. Psalm 116:15 says, "Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints." Precious means costly. It means weighted. It means God does not watch His people die the way the world watches — with indifference and a scroll. He watches with the attention of a Father who knows the number of hairs on every head. The death of a saint is not waste. It is the completion of a good work that God began and God finished.

The church's response is not to mourn as those who have no hope. It is to remember. Hebrews 13:3 commands: "Remember the prisoners as if chained with them — those who are mistreated — since you yourselves are in the body also." The body is not a metaphor. It is a reality. When Pastor Lora was shot in Cesar, the church in Los Angeles was wounded. When the seven pastors were buried in Guaviare, the church in Lagos was diminished. The Colombian government's decision to remove religious leaders from the protection list is not a Colombian problem. It is a body problem. The church that does not feel the blow is not a church. It is a social club. The CSW campaign is right to petition the next government. But the deeper petition is the one the church makes every time it gathers: we are one body, and when one member suffers, all suffer.

The final word is not grief. It is justice. Jesus told a parable about a widow who kept demanding vindication from an unjust judge. "And shall God not avenge His own elect who cry out day and night to Him, though He bears long with them? I tell you that He will avenge them speedily" (Luke 18:7-8). The judge in the parable was corrupt. The Judge who answers the elect is not. He bears long with them — the Greek word means to delay, to exercise patience, to wait for the full measure. But the delay is not denial. Speedily means suddenly. It means the day will come when the guerrilla commander who ordered the mass grave stands before a tribunal that does not permit trials in absentia, and the verdict will be final, and the appeal will be exhausted. No human petition can produce that day. No election can hasten it. Only God can. And He will.

The foundation holds its seal: "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19). The Colombian government does not know those who are His. The guerrilla group does not know those who are His. But the Lord knows. He knew Pastor Lora. He knew Pastor García. He knew Pastor Ortega. He knew the seven whose names may never appear in an international report. He knows them because He chose them before the guerrilla group chose them, before the government removed them from a list, before the first shot was fired. The foundation stands. The church in Colombia stands. The gates of Hades — armed, organized, and masked — shall not prevail against it.

Eleven pastors killed in Colombia since December 2024. The government removed them from the protection list. The guerrilla groups removed them from the earth. But the Lord knows those who are His — and not one of them fell apart from the Father's will.

Salvation

Union with Christ is not a metaphor. It is the channel through which every blessing flows.

Ask the average Christian how they are saved, and you will hear a list. Ask what connects those blessings to their actual life, and the answer gets quieter.
Covenant

The Bible is not sixty-six books that happen to share a binding.

Most readers treat the Bible like an anthology. The Bible itself has never read it that way.
Justice

Thirty-six nations signed a treaty to prosecute Putin. God signed none. He didn't need to.

Thirty-six nations created a special tribunal at The Hague to prosecute Vladimir Putin for the crime of aggression against Ukraine. Romans 13 says the magistrate bears the sword as God's minister. Amos 1 says God judges nations for war crimes without waiting for a signed treaty. Every human court is provisional. Every judge will be judged.
Family

Some parents break their children with harshness. Some break them with neglect. The Bible forbids both.

Sixty years of secular research landed on a formula: warmth plus boundaries equals flourishing kids. Proverbs wrote it down 3,000 years ago. Ephesians added what the research cannot: children are image-bearers AND sinners, and the only power that sustains loving authority is grace.
Ecclesiology

Rome threatened excommunication. The SSPX shrugged. Neither asked the right question.

The Vatican issued a final warning to SSPX: consecrate four new bishops without papal consent and incur automatic excommunication. The SSPX plans to proceed. Both sides assume someone other than Christ is Head of the church. The Reformation called this wrong in 1521. It's still wrong.
Persecution

They walked into a conflict zone to make peace. They were killed doing it.

Three Kuki Baptist church leaders were killed in Manipur while carrying a mandate for reconciliation between ethnic rivals. Jesus said peacemakers are called sons of God. The world kills them. God calls them family.
Ecclesiology

Egypt legalized 191 churches. Christ authorized one.

Egypt approved legal status for 191 more churches this week. Good news. But the church of Jesus Christ does not need Cairo's permission to exist. Christ authorized the church at Calvary, commissioned it at the Great Commission, and will present it to Himself in glory. No government participates in any of those acts.

What this is

A few people with full-time jobs and full lives who sat under sound preaching and wanted to share it with friends — without sending ninety-minute sermons or walls of text no one would read. Doctrine meant to be thought with, not just recited.

What it isn't

Not a devotional. Not a church bulletin. Not a news aggregator. We're not pastors, theologians, or influencers. We don't chase trends or write for engagement. We write for readers who don't yet share the frame, alongside those who do.

How it's checked

Every piece is sourced against Scripture and primary texts, read as a secularist would read it, and checked against the experience of someone who left an abusive church. If it fails any gate, it goes back to draft. Read how we verify.
