Day 6 of 365 · January

The Fall

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

The LORD said to Cain, "Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it."

Genesis 4:6-7

The first murder happened one generation from Eden. Cain and Abel brought offerings to the Lord. Abel brought the firstborn of his flock. Cain brought fruit of the ground. God regarded Abel's offering and not Cain's. The text does not say why — Hebrews 11:4 fills the gap: "By faith Abel offered a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain." Faith was the difference, not the content of the offering. Cain's response reveals the posture of his heart. Instead of repentance, he chose resentment. Instead of humility, he chose homicide.

God's warning to Cain is one of the most penetrating lines in Scripture: "Sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it" (Genesis 4:7). Sin is pictured as a predator, coiled and waiting. It is not a passive weakness. It is an active, hostile power seeking to dominate. The language echoes God's words to Eve in 3:16 — "your desire shall be contrary to your husband, but he shall rule over you." Sin relates to the sinner the way fallen humanity relates to fallen humanity: a struggle for mastery. You do not manage sin. You either rule over it, or it devours you.

Cain's punishment was not death — God placed a mark on him to protect him from vengeance. Even the first murderer received mercy. But he was sent away from the presence of the Lord, and the rest of his genealogy in Genesis 4:17-24 traces a line of cultural achievement (the first city, the father of music, the forger of bronze) alongside deepening moral decay. Lamech, the seventh from Adam in Cain's line, boasts of killing a man for wounding him and a boy for striking him. Civilization apart from God is not progress. It is amplification of the same rebellion with better tools. The chapter closes with a flicker of hope: "At that time people began to call upon the name of the Lord" (Genesis 4:26). Even in the darkness of Cain's line, there is a remnant.

Life Lesson

Sin is not passive weakness. It is an active predator seeking mastery. You either rule over it, or it rules over you. The first sign sin is winning is resentment toward someone God has blessed.
