Day 8 of 365 · January

Sin spreads

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain, saying, "I have gotten a man with the help of the LORD." And again, she bore his brother Abel. Now Abel was a keeper of sheep, and Cain a worker of the ground. In the course of time Cain brought to the LORD an offering of the fruit of the ground, and Abel also brought of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions. And the LORD had regard for Abel and his offering, but for Cain and his offering He had no regard.

Genesis 4:1-5

Genesis 4 is the first chapter after the Fall, and it reads like a post-mortem on everything that broke. Adam and Eve have children. One offers a pleasing sacrifice. The other does not. God warns Cain that "sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it" (Genesis 4:7). Cain ignores the warning. Within verses, the first murder in human history is committed — brother killing brother. Sin does not arrive fully grown. It crouches. It waits. It pounces. The story of the world after Eden is the story of sin spreading faster than anyone expected.

The Reformed tradition takes sin seriously because Genesis takes sin seriously. Total depravity does not mean that every person is as evil as they could possibly be. It means that sin has touched every part of every person — mind, will, affections — and that apart from grace, no one seeks God (Romans 3:10-11). The trajectory from Adam to Cain to the flood is the Bible's clearest demonstration of this doctrine. One generation. That is all it took for Paradise to become a crime scene. Do not underestimate what sin can do with a single generation.

Romans 5:12 traces the genealogy of sin back to its source: "Just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned." The problem is not merely that Cain killed Abel. The problem is that Cain is every man, and every man is Cain — unless grace intervenes. The opening chapters of Genesis are not ancient history. They are the operating manual for understanding the world you wake up in every morning. Sin spreads. Grace spreads further. But only grace.

Life Lesson

Sin is never static. Left unchecked, it spreads from thought to word to deed to destruction. Take your sin to God while it is still small. Do not wait until it grows teeth.
