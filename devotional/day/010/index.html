Noah's covenant

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

Then God said to Noah and to his sons with him, "Behold, I establish My covenant with you and your offspring after you, and with every living creature that is with you, the birds, the livestock, and every beast of the earth with you, as many as came out of the ark; it is for every beast of the earth. I establish My covenant with you, that never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the flood, and never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth."

Genesis 9:8-11

God's first words after the flood are not a warning. They are a promise. Genesis 9:8-17 records the Noahic covenant — God's unilateral pledge never to destroy the earth again by flood. This is remarkable for several reasons. First, God makes the covenant not only with Noah but with "every living creature." The animals are included. The earth itself is included. Second, the covenant has no conditions. God does not say "if you behave, I will not flood the earth." He simply says "I establish My covenant." The Hebrew verb for "establish" is the same one used for God's later covenant with Abraham. The same God who promised never to flood the earth promised to make Abraham's descendants as numerous as the stars.

The sign of the covenant is the rainbow. But the Hebrew word qeshet is the word for a battle bow — the weapon of war. God says, "I have set My bow in the cloud" (Genesis 9:13). The bow is pointed upward, away from the earth. God has laid down His weapon. He is not hostile to creation. He is committed to it. Every covenant in Scripture builds on this one — Abraham, Moses, David, the New Covenant in Christ. God binds Himself by promise, and every subsequent covenant is an expansion of His commitment to redeem what sin has broken. The Reformed confessions recognize this as the foundation of all covenant theology: God relates to His creation by covenant, not by contract.

Isaiah 54:9 later compares God's faithfulness to the Noahic covenant: "This is like the days of Noah to Me: as I swore that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, so I have sworn that I will not be angry with you." God's covenant faithfulness is the anchor of the Christian life. Your feelings fluctuate. Your circumstances change. Your faith wavers. God's covenant does not. The bow is still in the sky. The promise still stands. And the same God who promised not to flood the earth has promised not to cast out anyone who comes to Him through Christ (John 6:37).

Life Lesson

God's promises are more durable than your feelings. The next time you see a rainbow, remember: God put His weapon down. He is not waiting to destroy you. He is waiting to redeem you.
