Day 28 of 365 · January

Visit of the Magi

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

Now after Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of Herod the king, behold, wise men from the east came to Jerusalem, saying, 'Where is He who has been born king of the Jews? For we saw His star when it rose and have come to worship Him.' When they saw the star, they rejoiced exceedingly with great joy. And going into the house, they saw the child with Mary His mother, and they fell down and worshiped Him. Then, opening their treasures, they offered Him gifts — gold, frankincense, and myrrh.

Matthew 2:1-2, 10-11

The Magi are the most unexpected worshippers in the New Testament. They are Gentiles, astrologers from Persia or Babylon, practitioners of a discipline condemned in the Old Testament (Deuteronomy 18:10-12). They read stars, and God used a star to bring them to Christ. This is extraordinary condescension. God speaks to people in the language they understand, even when that language is flawed. The Magi followed the light they had, and God gave them more light — a star that led them to the Light of the world. The inclusion of the Magi in Matthew's birth narrative is a preview of the Great Commission: the gospel is for the nations, and the nations will come.

The contrast between the Magi and the Jewish religious establishment is deliberate and devastating. The Magi travel hundreds of miles to worship a Jewish infant they have never met. Herod and "all Jerusalem with him" (Matthew 2:3) are troubled by the news of a Messiah. The chief priests and scribes correctly identify Bethlehem as the birthplace from Micah 5:2, but none of them walk the six miles to see if the prophecy has been fulfilled. Knowledge without worship is dead orthodoxy. The Magi had less theology and more worship. They brought gifts — gold for a King, frankincense for a Priest, myrrh that foreshadows a burial. They did not bring opinions. They brought treasure. Worship costs something, or it is not worship.

The star is a sign that God governs the cosmos for the sake of the gospel. The same God who spoke stars into existence in Genesis 1 placed a particular star in the sky to guide particular Gentiles to a particular Child in a particular house in Bethlehem. The star was not a universal sign visible to everyone. It was a specific communication to specific people, and they responded. God's guidance is personal, not generic. The Magi were "warned in a dream not to return to Herod" and "departed to their own country by another way" (Matthew 2:12). Everyone who meets Christ goes home by another way. The old route is closed. The Magi returned to Persia, but they returned as different men. Worship changes the worshipper.

Life Lesson

The first worshippers of Jesus were outsiders. If you feel like an outsider — not raised in the church, not sure you belong — you are in good company. God draws worshippers from unexpected places. The Magi's journey is your invitation.
