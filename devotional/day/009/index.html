The flood and judgment

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. And the LORD regretted that He had made man on the earth, and it grieved Him to His heart. So the LORD said, "I will blot out man whom I have created from the face of the land, man and animals and creeping things and birds of the heavens, for I am sorry that I have made them." But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD.

Genesis 6:5-8

The flood narrative is one of the hardest texts in Scripture for modern readers to sit with. God destroys nearly all life on earth. Men, women, children, animals — wiped out. The text does not soften this. It does not apologize for it. Genesis 6:5 gives the reason with brutal clarity: "every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." The Hebrew piles words together for emphasis — every intention, only evil, all the day. This is not a bad week. This is total moral collapse. The flood is judgment. But it is not capricious judgment. It is what happens when a holy God confronts unrepentant evil.

But verse 8 changes everything: "But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD." The Hebrew word is chen — grace, unmerited favor. Noah was not sinless. He was a sinner who received grace. The same pattern repeats through all of Scripture: judgment is deserved, grace is given, and the recipient of grace does nothing to earn it. The flood is a preview of the final judgment. Jesus Himself makes the connection explicit: "As were the days of Noah, so will be the coming of the Son of Man" (Matthew 24:37). The ark was a physical salvation from physical judgment. Christ is the spiritual ark — and the judgment He saves from is eternal.

Genesis 8:1 contains one of the most important verses in the flood narrative, easily missed: "But God remembered Noah." This is not the memory of a forgetful deity who suddenly recalls something He misplaced. This is covenant remembrance — God acting on His promises. The same God who remembered Noah remembered His covenant with Abraham (Exodus 2:24) and remembered the thief on the cross (Luke 23:42). When God remembers you, He acts. The flood teaches both fear and hope: fear, because God judges sin; hope, because God provides the ark.

Life Lesson

God's judgment is real, and so is His mercy. The same God who sent the flood also sealed the ark. Do not presume upon either. Walk in the reverence that remembers both.
