Day 22 of 365 · January

Joseph sold

God of Beginnings & Sovereign PurposeOrdinary Time

Now Israel loved Joseph more than any other of his sons, because he was the son of his old age. And he made him a robe of many colors. But when his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him and could not speak peacefully to him. They said to one another, 'Here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits.'

Genesis 37:3-4, 19-20

Joseph's story takes up more of Genesis than any other single narrative — chapters 37 through 50 — and it begins with a seventeen-year-old boy in a pit. Joseph is Jacob's favorite son, the firstborn of the beloved Rachel, marked by a multicolored robe that announces his status. He has dreams of his brothers bowing to him and tells them about it — which is either remarkable honesty or remarkable social blindness, or both. The brothers hate him enough to kill him. Reuben intervenes. Judah proposes a compromise: sell him to Midianite traders for twenty shekels of silver — the price of a slave. They dip the robe in goat's blood and let Jacob believe his favorite son has been torn apart by wild animals. The pit is dark. The betrayal is complete. And God says nothing. Not a word from heaven. The silence of God is the loudest character in Genesis 37.

But Psalm 105:17 looks back on this moment and supplies what Genesis 37 withholds: "He had sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave." Who sent Joseph? The Midianites? The brothers? The text says God. God sent Joseph to Egypt. The brothers sold Joseph because they were jealous and wicked — their moral agency is not erased. But God's agency operates through their agency. God's plan rides on the backs of human decisions, including wicked ones. This is the mystery of divine sovereignty: God governs all events, including sinful human choices, without being the author of sin. Joseph's path to Egypt went through a pit dug by his brothers' hands, and both the hands and the pit were under God's control.

Joseph's descent into Egypt follows a pattern that the New Testament will make explicit: the Son is sent by the Father, rejected by His brothers, sold for silver, stripped of His robe, lowered into a pit, falsely accused, and eventually exalted to the right hand of power. Stephen makes the connection in Acts 7:9: "The patriarchs, jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt; but God was with him." God was with him — in the pit, in the caravan, in the slave market. The presence of God is not measured by the absence of suffering. Joseph's suffering was not evidence that God had abandoned him. It was evidence that God was using him. The pit is not the end. It is the first step of the path God has prepared.

Life Lesson

God's plan often looks like a disaster in the middle chapters. Joseph in the pit had no idea he would rule Egypt. Trust the Author when you cannot see the plot. The pit is not the end of the story. It is the beginning.
