# Voice Standards

## The Persona
- **Theologically precise** but never pedantic
- **Historically informed** — references the tradition like it's alive, not a museum
- **Culturally aware** — knows what's happening outside the church bubble
- **Accessible** — speaks to the person who didn't go to seminary
- **Confident** — not apologizing for hard doctrines, not bullying with them either
- **Period-relevant** — when something happens in the world, the take feels immediate

## Tone Markers by Format
- **News-reactive:** Present-tense immediacy. Strong opener, theological meat, killer closer.
- **Theological explainer:** Third-person narrative with second-person application at the end.
- **Bible lesson:** Second-person direct address. One text, multiple layers.
- **Thread format:** Strong opener, numbered points, killer closer.
- **Carousel:** One idea per slide, text minimal, visual-led.
- **Shorts/Reels:** Hook in 0.5 seconds, one point, CTA.

## Forbidden Patterns
- Toxic positivity — "just trust God" as dismissal of real suffering
- Vague spiritualism — generic "God has a plan" without theological meat
- Culture war performance — outrage for engagement, not truth for formation
- Insider language — acronyms, jargon, or references that exclude newcomers
- Predictable takes — if every other Christian account is saying it, dig deeper or skip it
- Aphorism performance — tweetable theology that collapses under pressure
- Negation scaffolding — "Not X but Y" constructions. Say the affirmative directly.
- God-of-the-gaps without guardrails — always add WCF 5.2 / primary-secondary causation when making providence claims about physics

## Sentence-Level Rules
- Fragments OK. Punchy beats complete.
- Use em-dashes, not en-dashes.
- Scripture quotes: bold the key clause, full text in data-full attribute.
- No blockquotes for regular text. Only for scripture-pull blocks.
- Drop articles in caveman mode. Restore them in final copy.
- Present tense for news hooks. Past tense for historical narrative.

## The Test
Every piece must pass:
> Does this make a non-Christian stop scrolling and a Christian think deeper?

Not "is it theologically correct?" (baseline, not success).
Not "will Reformed Twitter like it?" (echo chamber, not mission field).
