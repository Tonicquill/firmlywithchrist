The best news ever told

The Gospel

Introducing someone to Christ — a Bible-only guide.

No denominations. No traditions. Just Scripture. Who Jesus is, why He matters, and what it means to follow Him.

Part One: The Core

1. Who Is Jesus Christ — And Why Does He Matter?

Jesus Christ is God in human flesh. "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1). "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3). "He is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature, and he upholds the universe by the word of his power" (Hebrews 1:3). Thomas said it directly: "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28).

He is the only way to God. "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (John 14:6). This is exclusive. It is supposed to be. The gospel is not one path among many — it is the path.

The gospel in three parts: "Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures... he was buried... he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).

2. The Human Condition: Why We Need Saving

"All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). Not some. Not most. All. "None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one does good, not even one" (Romans 3:10-12).

Sin is not just doing bad things. It is a condition. We are born into it. "I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me" (Psalm 51:5). Left to ourselves, we don't seek God — we run from him. "Your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God" (Isaiah 59:2).

We cannot fix ourselves. "Can the Ethiopian change his skin or the leopard his spots? Then also you can do good who are accustomed to do evil" (Jeremiah 13:23). "You were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked" (Ephesians 2:1). Dead people can't do anything. They need someone else to act. That someone is God.

This sounds harsh. It is actually good news — because it means the pressure is not on you to be good enough. You can't be. That's the point.

3. What Jesus Accomplished on the Cross

He took our place. "God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Isaiah saw it 700 years before: "He was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities... the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:5-6).

He canceled our debt. "Having forgiven us all our trespasses, by canceling the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands. This he set aside, nailing it to the cross" (Colossians 2:13-14).

He defeated death. "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17). The resurrection is not a metaphor. It is physical, historical. The tomb was empty. "Death is swallowed up in victory. O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?" (1 Corinthians 15:54-55).

The cross is not just about going to heaven when you die. It is about a new life starting now. "We too might walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:4).

4. What Must a Person Do to Be Saved?

1

Repent

"Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" (Matthew 4:17). Repentance is not feeling bad — it is turning away. A change of direction. A change of mind.

→
2

Believe

"Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved" (Acts 16:31). Faith is not intellectual agreement — it is trust. Staking your life on Christ.

→
3

Confess

"If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord... you will be saved" (Romans 10:9). Saying out loud what you believe. Jesus is Lord.

→
4

Be Baptized

"Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ" (Acts 2:38). The first act of obedience. Public declaration.

"For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast" (Ephesians 2:8-9). Faith itself is a gift. You don't earn it. You receive it.

5. What Happens After Coming to Christ?

You become a new creation. "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (2 Corinthians 5:17). Not self-improvement. Re-creation.

You receive the Holy Spirit. "In him you also... were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance" (Ephesians 1:13-14). God living inside every believer — teaching, convicting, comforting, transforming.

You become part of the church. "They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers" (Acts 2:42). The Christian life is not solo.

You have assurance. "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. I give them eternal life, and they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand" (John 10:27-29). "Neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come... will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 8:38-39).

The Full Biblical Storyline

The entire Bible tells one story — a single, unified narrative of redemption.

Creation

Genesis 1-2

God spoke everything into existence. Everything was good. Humanity lived in perfect relationship with God, with each other, and with creation. No death. No suffering. No shame.

Fall

Genesis 3

Humanity rebelled. Sin entered the world. So did death, shame, and separation. But even in judgment, God made a promise: an offspring would one day crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15) — the first hint of the gospel.

The Patriarchs

Genesis 12-50

God called Abraham and made an impossible promise: "In you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness (Genesis 15:6). Faith — not works, not law.

Exodus and the Law

Exodus-Deuteronomy

God delivered His people from Egypt. At Sinai, He gave the Law — not to earn salvation, but as a mirror to show sin and a tutor pointing to Christ (Galatians 3:24). Sin requires death, but God accepts a substitute.

Kings and Prophets

Joshua-Malachi

The kingdom rose and fell. Through exile and return, God kept speaking through prophets. Isaiah saw the suffering servant (Isaiah 53). Jeremiah promised a new covenant (Jeremiah 31). Every prophet pointed forward. Someone was coming.

The Coming of Christ

Matthew-John

"When the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son" (Galatians 4:4-5). Jesus — born in Bethlehem, sinless life, authoritative teaching, crucified under Pontius Pilate, risen on the third day. The worst atrocity in human history was simultaneously the greatest act of love.

The Church Age

Acts-Revelation

From 120 believers in an upper room to the ends of the earth. Paul's letters systematically explain the gospel. Peter, John, James, and the author of Hebrews each contributed their witness. The New Testament closes with a vision of the end — and a beginning that never ends.

New Creation

Revelation 21-22

"Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth... He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore" (Revelation 21:1,4). The curse reversed. God dwells with His people. Face to face. Forever.

Part Two: How to Have This Conversation

Where Christians have failed

Christians have often been the worst advertisement for Christianity. Jesus warned about this — "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs which indeed appear beautiful outwardly, but inside are full of dead men's bones" (Matthew 23:27-28). The Crusades happened. Christians used "put your sword away" (Matthew 26:52) as a battle cry. Slavery was justified from Scripture. Residential schools traumatized indigenous children.

If someone has been hurt by Christians: don't defend it. Don't explain it away. Listen. Acknowledge. Then point, gently, to Jesus — who is not the same thing as the people who claim His name. "Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick" (Mark 2:17). Admitting the church is sick clears the path to the Physician.

Practical guidance

1. Pray first. Before you say anything to them, say something to God. This is His work, not yours.
2. Listen. "Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak" (James 1:19). Ask questions. Listen because they are a person, not a project.
3. Let the Bible speak. "Faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ" (Romans 10:17). Your arguments won't save anyone. God's Word does the work.
4. Be honest about what you don't know. "I don't know" builds trust. Fake confidence doesn't.
5. Don't pressure. You cannot argue, guilt, or scare someone into genuine faith.
6. If ready — lead simply. Do they believe Jesus is who He claimed to be? Are they willing to turn from sin and trust Him? Are they ready to confess Him publicly? Pray with them.
7. If not ready — love anyway. The relationship matters more than the timeline.
8. Don't abandon them. A newborn Christian is a spiritual infant. Help them get a Bible, find a church, connect with believers, start praying.

Starting points for reading

For someone new: Start with the Gospel of Mark (shortest, fastest-moving), then John (deepest theology), then Romans (Paul's full explanation of the gospel).

For you — to go deeper: The Gospel of John (who Jesus claimed to be), Romans 1-8 (sin, salvation, Christian life), Ephesians 1-2 (salvation by grace through faith), Isaiah 52:13-53:12 (the suffering servant, 700 years before Christ).

The Root Principle

Every conversation about Christ must pass one test:
Are you sharing good news or winning an argument?

If you are trying to win, you have already lost the plot.
