China freed two pastors. The word of God was never chained.

Pastor Li Jie and Han Xiaodong walked out of a Chinese prison after nearly four years. Their crime: preaching Christ without a state permit. What man meant for evil, God meant for the furtherance of the gospel.

Persecution China Genesis 50:20 1 Peter 5:10 2 Timothy 2:9-10 Philippians 1:12 Psalm 40:1-3 Acts 5:29

Pastor Li Jie and Han Xiaodong walked out of a Chinese prison this week after nearly four years. Their crime was "fraud" — the state's preferred charge for house church leaders who refuse to register with the government-controlled Three-Self Patriotic Movement. The real offense was preaching Christ outside the state's permission structure. They were seized in a 2022 police raid on an outdoor Christian camp in Linfen City. Now they are free. The state meant it for evil. The question the global church must ask is what God meant.[1]

Joseph's brothers threw him in a pit, sold him to slavers, and told their father he was dead. Years later, standing before those same brothers with the power of Egypt in his hand, Joseph said the thing that reframes every imprisonment of God's people: "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good" (Genesis 50:20). The Chinese state meant the dungeon for evil. God meant it for the preservation of testimony. The Reformed tradition, echoing the whole counsel of God, teaches that "God positively ordains whatsoever comes to pass" and "harnesses the deeds of the wicked" to accomplish His purposes. The prison cell was never outside His jurisdiction. It was a refiners' fire — designed not to destroy these pastors but to "perfect, confirm, strengthen and establish" them (1 Peter 5:10).

The word that no cell can hold

I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained.

2 Timothy 2:9

Paul wrote 2 Timothy 2:9–10 from a Roman prison cell. He calls himself an "evildoer" — the same category the Chinese state applied to Li Jie and Han Xiaodong. The charge is the same across centuries. The response is the same: "the word of God is not chained." You can lock up the preacher. You cannot lock up the Word. Paul's chains, he wrote elsewhere, "actually turned out for the furtherance of the gospel" (Philippians 1:12). The guards who watched him heard the gospel. The officials who processed his case encountered Christ. The church outside prayed harder because their apostle was inside.

Four years in a Chinese prison is not a missional failure. It is an apostolic credential. The church that calls itself the church militant does not get to be surprised when the battle costs casualties. But it also does not get to despair when those casualties walk free — because the same sovereignty that ordained the imprisonment ordained the release.

He brought me out of the miry clay

Psalm 40 gives the song of the released: "I waited patiently for the LORD; and He inclined to me, and heard my cry. He also brought me up out of a horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my steps. He has put a new song in my mouth — praise to our God; many will see it and fear, and will trust in the LORD" (Psalm 40:1–3). The psalmist insists that praise is an evangelistic tool. When the world sees a believer emerge from the pit singing, "many will see it and fear, and will trust in the LORD." Li Jie and Han Xiaodong have been given a new song. The question is whether the global church will sing it with them or scroll past.

This is not a moment for naive triumphalism. The Chinese house church remains under pressure. The Three-Self Patriotic Movement still functions as a state apparatus for controlling Christian worship. Other pastors remain imprisoned. Advocacy groups warn that persecution in China is ongoing, not resolved. But the release of these two men is a crack of light, and Reformed theology knows what to do with cracks of light: it calls them signals of the coming dawn. "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). Li Jie and Han Xiaodong obeyed. The state punished. God released. The arc is not a political cycle. It is a divine trajectory — and it ends at the resurrection.

The Chinese state locked up two pastors for four years. Their crime: preaching Christ without a state permit. Now they are free. The word of God was never chained. It never is.
