They submitted every document. The permit never came.

160 Christians in East Kalimantan did everything the government asked. The permit never came. The Reformers would recognize this.

Ecclesiology Indonesia Acts 5:29 Isaiah 33:22 1 Samuel 2:7 Matthew 16:18 1 Peter 2:5 Hebrews 10:25

The Toraja Church in Samarinda Seberang, East Kalimantan, has 160 members. They obtained a recommendation from the Interfaith Harmony Forum in September 2024. They secured approval from the Ministry of Religious Affairs in April 2025. Every document the Indonesian government asked for, they submitted. The permit never came.[1]

In Teluknaga, the Thessalonika Christian Ecumenical Fellowship Church was sealed after Good Friday 2026 — the second consecutive Easter the congregation has faced eviction.[2] In Riau, the HKBP Kulim Jaya Church faced protests for renovating a crumbling building.[3] The pattern: submit the documents, wait, face opposition, wait some more, worship in borrowed rooms while bureaucracy decides whether the church belongs.

The Pharaoh Playbook

When Moses told Pharaoh that Israel needed to go worship their God, Pharaoh didn't say no. He said: "You shall lay on them the quota of bricks which they made before. You shall not reduce it. For they are idle; therefore they cry out, saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God'" (Exodus 5:7–8). He didn't deny the request. He buried it under more work. Cut the straw supply, raise the brick quota, make worship impossible by making life impossible.

Every Indonesian congregation sitting in permit limbo knows this tactic. The state doesn't say "no." It says "submit more documents." It says "the community objects." It says "not yet." The effect is the same as Pharaoh's brick quota: the church stays provisional, exhausting itself on paperwork while the question hangs unanswered — are we allowed to be here?

The Decree That Actually Matters

When the Jews returned from exile to rebuild the temple, hostile neighbors filed complaints with the Persian court. The project stalled for sixteen years. But Ezra records how it finally finished: "So the elders of the Jews built, and they prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. And they built and finished it, according to the commandment of the God of Israel, and according to the command of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes king of Persia" (Ezra 6:14).

Look at the order. The "commandment of the God of Israel" comes first. The decrees of three of the greatest monarchs in history are listed second — as instrumental means, not ultimate authority. God's decree drove the building. The Persian permits served it.

The Toraja Church already has its Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes decrees: the IFH recommendation, the Ministry approval. Those are real and legitimate. But the only decree that matters was given on a Galilean hillside: "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth. Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations" (Matthew 28:18–20). That is the permit. It was issued by the one who holds all authority. It has never been revoked. It does not require a zoning board's countersignature.

Pray for the Ones Blocking the Permit

When the Sanhedrin commanded Peter and John to stop preaching, they answered: "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you more than to God, you judge. For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard" (Acts 4:19–20). Then they prayed — not for the Sanhedrin's destruction, but for boldness to keep preaching (Acts 4:29–31).

Paul, writing under Nero's regime — knowing Nero would eventually execute him — commanded something even more radical: "Therefore I exhort first of all that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks be made for all men, for kings and all who are in authority, that we may lead a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and reverence" (1 Timothy 2:1–4). Pray for the officials blocking your permit. Pray for the magistrate who won't sign. Pray for the community leaders filing objections. This is not passivity. It is the church's first weapon — the one that outlasts every empire.

Keep Knocking

Jesus told a parable about a widow before an unrighteous judge — a man who "did not fear God nor regard man." She kept coming. He kept refusing. She kept coming. Eventually the judge relented, not from justice but from exhaustion at her persistence. Jesus' application: "Hear what the unrighteous judge said; now, will not God bring about justice for His elect who cry to Him day and night, and will He delay long over them? I tell you that He will bring about justice for them quickly" (Luke 18:6–8).

The widow didn't have power, money, or leverage. She had persistence. The unrighteous judge had a position, a bench, and a gavel. The widow won. The church in East Kalimantan has been submitting paperwork since September 2024. That is persistence. The question is whether their persistence is fueled by prayer or paperwork optimism. The widow didn't put her hope in the judge's goodness. She kept appealing to the only one who could grant relief — and she kept praying to the only One who controls even unrighteous judges.

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.

Matthew 28:19–20

An Indestructible Spiritual Temple

Christ's promise in Matthew 16:18 — that the gates of hell will not prevail against His church — is not a promise about building codes. It is a promise about ontology. The church is a spiritual house made of living stones (1 Peter 2:5). Municipal approval cannot create it. Municipal denial cannot destroy it. The congregation in a borrowed hall is as much the church as any cathedral, because Christ is as present in the borrowed hall as He is beneath any consecrated spire.

Nebuchadnezzar stood on his palace roof and boasted: "Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for a royal dwelling by my mighty power and for the honor of my majesty?" (Daniel 4:30–31). "While the word was still in the king's mouth," the kingdom departed. The greatest building project of the ancient Near East, and it evaporated while the builder was still admiring it. Every human monument goes the way of all flesh. The church is different. No wrecking ball can touch living stones.

The church does not need a permit to be the church. The command to gather issues from a throne no municipal council has ever approached. The permission that matters was given on a Galilean hillside. It has never been revoked.
