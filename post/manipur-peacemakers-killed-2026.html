They walked into a conflict zone to make peace. They were killed doing it.

Three Kuki Baptist church leaders were killed in Manipur while carrying a mandate for reconciliation between ethnic rivals. Jesus said peacemakers are called sons of God. The world kills them. God calls them family.

Persecution India Matthew 5:9 Genesis 4:10 Psalm 116:15 Hebrews 12:24 Colossians 1:20 Ephesians 2:14 Galatians 6:9 Revelation 21:4 2 Timothy 2:19

Three Kuki Baptist church leaders were killed in Manipur on Tuesday. Their vehicles were ambushed. They were not carrying weapons. They were carrying a mandate for reconciliation between the Kuki and Tangkhul Naga communities — ethnic rivals locked in a conflict that has killed over 260 people and displaced 60,000 since 2023.[1] The delegation came from the Thadou Baptist Association and the United Baptist Council. They were peacemakers. Someone decided peacemakers were a threat worth killing. The Evangelical Fellowship of India and the Baptist World Alliance condemned the attack. The world's press may not linger on it. Three more names in a conflict that has already produced too many names. But the church takes notice of peacemakers who die in the act of making peace. The church has a category for this. It is ancient. It runs from Genesis to Golgotha.

Jesus said something about peacemakers that the secular mind cannot process: "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God" (Matthew 5:9). Not "blessed are the peacekeepers" — those who maintain order by suppressing tension. Not "blessed are the peace-lovers" — those who feel warmly about harmony. Peacemakers. Active. Costly. The kind who walk into a conflict zone to reconcile enemies and get buried for it. Jesus calls them sons of God. And if the peacemakers are sons of God, then those who kill peacemakers are fighting against the family likeness of the Father Himself.

The blood that speaks better

And He said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground."

Genesis 4:10

Abel was not a peacemaker in the active sense. He was a worshiper whose offering God accepted. But his blood is the first blood to cry out from the earth in Scripture, and the pattern it establishes is irreversible: innocent blood has a voice, and God hears it. The blood of the Manipur peacemakers is not silent. It cries out from Indian soil. The Lord who heard Abel's blood will not fail to hear theirs. "Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of His saints" (Psalm 116:15). The Hebrew word for "precious" here means weighty, costly, not to be treated lightly. Every death of the faithful registers in the throne room of God with a weight that no news cycle can measure.

The writer of Hebrews draws the line from Abel's blood to Christ's: "Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" (Hebrews 12:24). Abel's blood cried out for justice. Christ's blood cries out that justice has been satisfied and mercy is now extended. The peacemakers of Manipur died testifying to a peace purchased by a peace they did not originate. They were extending horizontally what Christ accomplished vertically: reconciliation.

The peacemaker who died to make what He declared

This is the point that separates Christian peacemaking from every secular version. Secular peacemakers broker ceasefires. Christian peacemakers proclaim a peace that already exists — the peace Christ made "through the blood of His cross" (Colossians 1:20). The peace between God and sinners is accomplished. It is finished. The peacemaker's task is to announce it and to live in its implications — including the implication that the peace Christ made between Jew and Gentile is the model for every reconciliation across ethnic lines. "For He Himself is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in His flesh the dividing wall of hostility" (Ephesians 2:14).

The Manipur peacemakers understood this. Kuki and Tangkhul Naga are ethnic categories. In Christ, those categories remain culturally real but are spiritually superseded. Baptism does not erase ethnicity. It overrides it as a basis for hostility. The peacemakers who died in Manipur were not naive. They knew the risk. They walked into the zone of enmity because the enmity Christ abolished is the model for every lesser reconciliation. If Christ could reconcile Jew and Gentile — groups divided by millennia of religious, cultural, and political hostility — then Kuki and Tangkhul Naga can be reconciled. The peacemakers staked their lives on that theological premise. Someone staked bullets on the opposite one.

Peacemaking is not a safe activity that Christians occasionally pursue. It is a family trait. "They shall be called sons of God" means peacemaking is a visible marker of those who belong to the God of peace. The world kills peacemakers. God calls them His children.

The sons of God will be recognized

And let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart.

Galatians 6:9

Paul's encouragement is not sentimental. It is eschatological. "In due season" means the harvest is not now. The peacemakers were killed while doing good. They did not see the reconciliation they labored for. They reaped bullets, not peace. Paul's answer is not to promise better results next time. It is to point to a Day when the results will be rendered by the Judge of all the earth. On that Day, the peacemakers who were called naive, who were called traitors to their ethnic group, who were ambushed and killed — they will be called what Christ already called them: sons of God.

"He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away" (Revelation 21:4). The peacemakers of Manipur do not yet see this peace. They are with the Prince of Peace but the peace they died to make on earth remains unmade. The church does not pretend otherwise. The church grieves, prays, and continues the work — not because success is guaranteed in this age, but because the sons of God bear the family likeness. The Father is a reconciler. So are His children. Some of them die doing it. The foundation holds its seal: "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19). He knows the ones who fell in Manipur. He knows what they were doing. He knows why.
