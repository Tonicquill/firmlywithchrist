Son of Hamas. Son of God. The spy who loved not his life unto death.

Mosab Hassan Yousef was born into Hamas royalty — son of a founding leader. He became Israel's most valuable spy inside the organization, then a Christian. The bloodline could not save him. The blood of the Lamb could.

Conversion Palestine/Israel Acts 9:1 John 1:12-13 Matthew 10:37-38 2 Timothy 2:19 Genesis 50:20 Romans 5:10 Revelation 12:11 1 Corinthians 1:27

Mosab Hassan Yousef was born in 1978 into the house of his enemies — though he did not know it yet. His father, Sheikh Hassan Yousef, is a founding leader of Hamas, the Islamist militant organization that has waged war against Israel for decades and explicitly calls for the destruction of the Jewish state. The boy was raised inside the ideology the way a fish is raised in water — he did not know it was there. He breathed it. He absorbed the grammar of martyrdom, the glorification of death, the sacred duty of armed resistance. By his own account in later interviews, his childhood was a curriculum in radicalization. Then, as a young man, Israeli forces arrested him. Inside the prison, he witnessed something that cracked the entire edifice: Hamas members torturing fellow Palestinians suspected of collaboration. The enemy of his enemy was not his friend. The enemy of his enemy was a torturer who wore the same flag. The spell broke. He walked out of that prison, walked into a Shin Bet intelligence office, and became "The Green Prince" — the most valuable Israeli asset inside Hamas in the organization's history.[1][2]

The world has a category for this kind of man: defector, traitor, spy, whistleblower, apostate. Scripture has older and more precise categories. Saul of Tarsus was a Pharisee of Pharisees who "breathed threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord" (Acts 9:1) until Christ struck him blind on the Damascus road and turned the chief persecutor into the chief apostle. The trajectory is not accidental. It is archetypal. The enemy of God's people, when sovereignly converted, becomes the most potent witness — because he knows the darkness from the inside, and he knows exactly what it cost him to leave. John 1:12–13 provides the theological operating system: you are born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. Mosab's biological bloodline ran straight into the Hamas founding circle. His new birth runs somewhere else entirely. The Reformed tradition, reflecting on Matthew 10:37–38, has always taught that regeneration does not consult your family tree. The seal is not ancestral lineage. It is divine election. "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19).

The Green Prince: A decade inside the darkness

But as for you, you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good, in order to bring it about as it is this day, to save many people alive.

Genesis 50:20

Shin Bet, Israel's internal security service, gave him the codename "The Green Prince" — green for the Hamas flag, prince because his father was Hamas royalty. From approximately 1997 to 2007, Mosab fed Israeli intelligence information from inside his father's own organization. He prevented suicide bombings. He exposed terror cells. He identified Hamas operatives and their plans before they could execute. The intelligence he provided saved Israeli lives and Palestinian lives — because every thwarted attack meant one less family burying their dead on either side. The calculus is stark and unpoetic: a man raised to destroy Israel became one of the primary instruments that preserved it.

The pattern is Joseph, sold into Egypt by his own brothers, who rose to become the preserver of the very people who betrayed him. "Genesis 50:20" does not describe a comfortable, sanitized providence. It describes a violent and politically radioactive one — where God harnesses the deeds of the wicked, including the terrorist infrastructure of Hamas itself, to accomplish His good and perfect ends. The Westminster Confession of Faith (5.1) teaches that God "sovereignly orders everything He has made." Everything. The Hamas leadership structure. The prison cell where a young Mosab first witnessed his own side's brutality. The Shin Bet recruitment. The decade of covert operations. No link in the chain sat outside divine jurisdiction for a single second.

Mosab's intelligence work was not merely a geopolitical operation. It was a theological statement performed in flesh and blood and risk. Every suicide bombing prevented was a life preserved by the hidden hand of the God who declares, "The LORD kills and makes alive; He brings down to the grave and brings up" (1 Samuel 2:6). The insider who saves lives from within the enemy camp is a living parable of grace — the ultimate insider being Christ Himself, who entered enemy-occupied territory in human flesh to rescue His own. "For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God by the death of His Son, much more, now that we are reconciled, shall we be saved by His life" (Romans 5:10).

From Isa to Jesus: The conversion that cost everything

At some point during his years as an intelligence asset, Mosab began reading the Bible in secret. The Jesus he encountered in the Gospels bore no resemblance to the prophet Isa he had been taught about in Islam. One was a human teacher who did not die on a cross — the Quran explicitly denies the crucifixion. The other was the eternal Son of God, crucified for sins, risen from the dead, ascending to the right hand of the Father. The difference between these two figures is infinite. It is the difference between a religion of works and a gospel of grace. Mosab chose the Jesus of Scripture. He did not merely defect from Hamas. He defected from Islam. Then he defected from secrecy.

In 2008, Mosab went public — first about his intelligence work, then about his Christian faith. He published Son of Hamas in 2010, a memoir that laid out the entire arc: childhood radicalization, the prison awakening, the decade as The Green Prince, the secret conversion. The book made him an apostate under Islamic law — a capital offense. It made him a traitor to his father's legacy and his family's name. It severed him permanently from the community, culture, and kinship network he was born into. He did it anyway. "Revelation 12:11" names this posture exactly: they overcame by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, and they loved not their lives even unto death. Mosab did not seek martyrdom the way Hamas taught him to. He sought something harder: a living sacrifice — public, exposed, permanently hunted.

The voice that will not quiet: 2026

Now forty-seven years old, Mosab has not retreated into a quiet life in some Western suburb. He remains one of the most visible and uncomfortable voices in the conversation about Islamist terror, the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, and the nature of radicalization itself. His 2026 calendar alone tells the story.

In February 2026, he drew a sold-out crowd of two hundred people at the University of Miami for a Students Supporting Israel event moderated by Dr. Ira Sheskin. Tickets sold out in two days. The waitlist hit capacity. He used the platform to challenge Reza Pahlavi, the son of the last Iranian Shah, who has positioned himself as a democratic alternative to the Islamic Republic. Mosab was blunt: "He is presenting himself as this loving monarch. But show me his qualifications, because I do not see them." He also addressed Trump's controversial Gaza proposal and laid out the ideological architecture of Hamas's death cult from the inside — something no professor of Middle Eastern studies can replicate, because no professor lived it.[3]

That same month, Mosab sat for an hours-long livestreamed interview with the Ayn Rand Institute, hosted by Yaron Brook and Elan Journo. The framing was secular. The content was devastatingly theological. He described Hamas's ideology as built on "the glorification of death" and Israel's posture as rooted in the valuing of human life. He spoke of the "conscious moral choice" to protect life as the pivot point of his own transformation — a framework that any Reformed Christian should recognize as common grace breaking through, the image of God asserting itself against a system designed to erase it. He detailed Hamas's tactics of sexual repression, the subordination of women, and the manufacture of martyrs. These are not fringe observations from a commentator. They are eyewitness testimony from a man who was being groomed for leadership before he walked away.[2]

In March 2026, Mosab publicly challenged former President Trump's claims about confronting Iran. In a social media post that gathered tens of thousands of views, he wrote: "Mr. President, you have miserably failed to remove Hamas from power, what makes you believe you have what it takes to defeat the Islamic Republic?" The rebuke was not partisan. It was surgical — a former insider assessing a geopolitical posture and finding it hollow. He has also called for a peaceful Israeli counter-flotilla in response to the Global Sumud Flotilla of approximately fifty vessels attempting to break the Gaza blockade, among whose participants is climate activist Greta Thunberg. His instruction to Israelis: "No ramming, just presence." The man who once worked in the shadows now wages a public argument — for life, for truth, for the distinction between a culture that celebrates death and one that protects the living.[4][5]

Some Christians are uncomfortable with Mosab Hassan Yousef. He does not fit the mold of a conversion testimony. His politics are complicated. His theology is still forming rather than fully systematized. He does not speak in evangelical cliches. He speaks like a man who walked out of the darkness and knows exactly what the darkness costs — and he does not modulate his voice to make comfortable people more comfortable. The Reformed tradition has a category for this discomfort. It is the same discomfort the Jerusalem church felt about Saul of Tarsus: "They were all afraid of him, and did not believe that he was a disciple" (Acts 9:26). But God "has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty" (1 Corinthians 1:27). The son of a Hamas founder who becomes a Shin Bet spy and then a Christian is not a tidy testimony. It is a biblical one. The Bible is full of figures whose conversion arcs are messy and politically radioactive and exactly where God's glory lands hardest.

A boy raised in the house of God's enemies walked out of a prison, into a spy agency, through a secret conversion, and onto the public stage — where he remains in 2026, saying what no one else can say because no one else has lived it. The blood of the Lamb overcame. The word of his testimony is still speaking.
