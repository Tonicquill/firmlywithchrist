Manipur: 20 Christians Still Held Hostage. The Lord Was With Joseph in Prison.

Twenty Christians remain hostage in Manipur, India — fourteen Kuki and six Naga believers held in ethnic violence that has already killed three pastors. The wives of the slain pastors were released. Twenty believers were not. God is already in the cell with them.

Persecution India Genesis 39:21 Psalm 139:8 Romans 8:35

On May 27, International Christian Concern confirmed that twenty Christians remain hostage in Manipur, India — fourteen Kuki and six Naga believers held by armed groups in a state where ethnic violence has already killed three pastors this month. The abductions began after militants murdered three Kuki-Thadou pastors returning from the United Baptist Convention Assembly 2026, a peace conference focused on reconciliation between the Kuki-Zo and Naga tribes. Armed Kuki groups abducted Naga civilians. Naga armed groups retaliated by abducting Kuki civilians, including the wives of the three slain pastors. On May 16, negotiations led by church and tribal leaders secured the release of fourteen Naga and fourteen Kuki civilians. The pastors' wives were among those freed. But twenty believers remain captive. And a lack of information about the six Naga hostages has led some to fear they are already dead. Christian Solidarity Worldwide President Mervyn Thomas condemned the 'targeted attacks on revered church leaders.' 21Wilberforce President Wissam al-Saliby called for peace: 'Our identity in Christ must be stronger than tribal or ethnic divisions.' The state has been under President's Rule since February. The army patrols. The politicians negotiate. And twenty Christians sit in darkness while the world scrolls past.[1]

The narrator of Genesis records what happened after Potiphar's wife falsely accused Joseph and had him thrown into prison: "But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him mercy, and He gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison" (Genesis 39:21). Joseph was not in prison because of his faith. He was in prison because of his righteousness. The same is true in Manipur. The twenty hostages are not captive because they sinned. They are captive because they belong to Christ in a state where that belonging has become a death sentence. The Kuki militant who abducted a Naga believer cannot abduct him from the hand of God. The Naga militant who retaliates against a Kuki civilian cannot retaliate against the mercy that God showed Joseph in Pharaoh's dungeon. The keeper of the prison does not know he is being watched by a higher Keeper.

But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him mercy, and He gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison.

Genesis 39:21

David wrote what every hostage needs to know: "If I make my bed in Sheol, behold, You are there" (Psalm 139:8). Sheol is the Old Testament word for the place of the dead — the darkness, the silence, the grave. But the psalmist does not say God visits Sheol. He says God is there. Present tense. The Manipur hostages have been taken to places the negotiators cannot find. Some fear the six Naga hostages have already been killed. But the church does not grieve as those who have no hope. If a hostage makes their bed in a militant camp, the Lord is there. If they make their bed in the grave, the Lord is there. The darkness is not dark to Him. The silence is not silent. The location that terrifies the hostage is the location that proves God's omnipresence.

Paul asked the Roman church: "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?" (Romans 8:35). The question is not rhetorical. It is a dare. Paul names the weapons that have been used against Christians from Nero to now — tribulation, distress, persecution, peril, sword — and asks if any of them can sever the bond between Christ and His own. The answer is implicit in the question. The Kuki militant with a gun cannot do it. The Naga militant with a grudge cannot do it. The ethnic division that has turned brother against brother in Manipur cannot do it. "In all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us" (Romans 8:37). The hostage who trusts Christ is not a victim of tribal politics. He is a conqueror whose conqueror is already seated at the right hand of God.

The world will forget these twenty hostages by next week. The United Baptist Convention Assembly that the three pastors attended was about reconciliation. Their killers made it about retaliation. And the retaliation spiraled into a hostage crisis that the Indian press barely covers and the international community will not act on. But the church has a different memory. The church remembers Joseph in prison. The church remembers Daniel in the lions' den. The church remembers the apostle Paul in chains, writing to the Philippians that his imprisonment has actually advanced the gospel. The church remembers that the Lord was with Joseph — not because Joseph was special, but because Joseph belonged to the God who does not abandon His own. The twenty hostages in Manipur belong to that same God. The militants who hold them have not taken prisoners. They have only taken Christians to a place where God already is. And that is not a prison. That is a testimony.

Twenty Christians remain hostage in Manipur. Fourteen Kuki. Six Naga. Three pastors already dead. The militants believe they have taken prisoners. They have only taken believers to a place where God already is. The Lord was with Joseph in prison. He is with the hostages in Manipur. And He will be with them when the door finally opens — or when it never does, and they see Him face to face instead.
