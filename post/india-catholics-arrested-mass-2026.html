India: Mob Disrupts Mass. Police Arrest the Catholics. Bail Denied Twice.

Nine Catholics in Rajasthan were arrested after confronting a mob that stormed their church during Mass. Police charged the victims with attempted murder and illegal conversion. Bail has been denied twice.

Persecution India Exodus 23:7 Acts 5:29 Hebrews 13:3

On May 1, a mob of Hindu activists stormed into a Catholic parish substation in Kalinjara village, Rajasthan, during evening Mass. They shouted accusations of conversion, brandished a knife, and filmed the congregation of about seventy Catholics. Parishioners confronted them, snatched the weapon, and chased them out. When police arrived, they arrested nine Catholics — four that night and five more in a pre-dawn raid on May 4 — and charged them with attempted murder and illegal conversion under the Rajasthan Prohibition of Unlawful Conversion of Religion Act, 2025. The mob was not charged. A magistrate denied bail on May 5. The district court denied it again on May 12.[1] The state did not fail to see the injustice. It chose the injustice.

God spoke to Moses: "Keep yourself far from a false matter; do not kill the innocent and righteous. For I will not justify the wicked" (Exodus 23:7). This is not a suggestion for personal ethics. It is a command to civil magistrates — a direct order to the ministers of God who bear the sword for good. The Rajasthan police did not merely mishandle a dispute. They inverted divine justice. A mob disrupted the worship of the living God, threatened the congregation with a blade, and walked free. The Catholics who defended their church were thrown into a legal system that under the state's 2025 anti-conversion law shifts the burden of proof onto the accused. The magistrate who denied bail was not exercising neutral jurisprudence. He was executing a law designed to criminalize the Christian faith itself.

Keep yourself far from a false matter; do not kill the innocent and righteous. For I will not justify the wicked.

Exodus 23:7

Peter and the apostles answered the Sanhedrin: "We ought to obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). The church in Kalinjara was not holding a political rally. It was celebrating the Eucharist — the central act of obedience Christ commanded His people to perform until He returns. The mob did not stumble upon the service by accident. It entered during the Communion rite, the holiest moment of the Mass, with cameras rolling and a knife brandished. The parishioners who chased them out were not rioters. They were stewards of a sacred space, obeying the God who says "do not forsake the assembling of ourselves together." When the state arrests Christians for defending worship and calls it attempted murder, it reveals whose authority it truly fears. It is not the mob. It is the God the mob came to silence.

The writer to the Hebrews commanded: "Remember the prisoners as if chained with them — those who are mistreated — since you yourselves are in the body also" (Hebrews 13:3). The nine Catholics in Banswara district sit in jail while the men who disrupted their worship post videos online. Among the arrested is a seventy-year-old retired government school headmaster — a man who spent his life serving the same state that now accuses him of conspiracy to convert. The church's response must be more than legal petitions. It must be spiritual solidarity. To remember the prisoners is to recognize that their chains are our chains, that the body of Christ is not divided by nationality or denomination, and that an assault on one congregation is an assault on the Head Himself.

The world will call this a local dispute. Hindu nationalists will call it a defense of culture. The Rajasthan government will call it law enforcement. But the charge sheet tells the truth: attempted murder, rioting, wrongful restraint, and illegal conversion — all filed against the congregation that was attacked. The same Jesus who promised that the gates of hell would not prevail against His church also promised that His people would be delivered up to councils, beaten in synagogues, and brought before rulers for His name's sake. The church in India has endured pogroms, church burnings, and anti-conversion laws for decades. It has not prevailed by avoiding persecution. It has prevailed by refusing to worship the state that persecutes it. The Catholics in Kalinjara will not be the first to face this choice, and they will not be the first to refuse.

A mob disrupted Mass in Rajasthan. The Catholics who defended their church were arrested, charged with attempted murder and illegal conversion, and denied bail twice. The state did not fail to see the injustice. It chose the injustice.
