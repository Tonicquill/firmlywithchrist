When a Christian Leader Forgets His Frame

Kenya's President Ruto was once seen as a devout Christian. Now megachurch pastors and Catholic archbishops are publicly rebuking him. 'Goonism' is spreading ahead of 2027 elections. What happens when a leader who professes Christ governs as though Christ never gave commands.

Politics Kenya Matthew 23:27-28 1 Samuel 15:22-23 Luke 16:13 Matthew 7:21-23 Titus 1:16

William Ruto was once known for prayer breakfasts, public Bible references, and evangelical language. He positioned himself as a Christian leader governing from faith. That was the brand. The brand is now in dispute.

Pastor Wilfred Lai — one of Kenya's most prominent megachurch leaders — publicly accused Ruto of abandoning Christian values. Catholic Archbishop Maurice Muhatia rebuked both Ruto and his former deputy for trading insults. These are not political critics. These are church leaders. "Goonism" — organized political violence using hired thugs — is spreading. Analysts warn the 2027 election could be "very bloody."

Whitewashed Tombs

Jesus addressed this directly: "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs which indeed appear beautiful outwardly, but inside are full of dead men's bones and all uncleanness" (Matthew 23:27–28). The whitewashed tomb is the most precise description of performative Christian leadership ever written. Outside: beautiful. Polished. Religious. Inside: death. The performance fools people. It does not fool God. Jesus "outspokenly denounced" those who "wear their religion on their sleeves" to "attract attention and bring honor to themselves." The Pharisees were the most meticulous religious performers in Israel. Jesus called them sons of hell.

Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams.

1 Samuel 15:22

The Cost of Disobedience in the Public Square

Saul was the original case study. God gave him the throne. Saul decided partial obedience was enough — kept the Amalekite king alive, kept the best livestock, then tried to cover it with religious ceremony. Samuel's verdict: "Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry" (1 Samuel 15:22–23).

Performance religion cannot substitute for obedience. Saul tried to use ritualized ceremony to cover self-styled disobedience. God removed him from the throne. The "record of history" shows that God "pulls kings from their thrones when they have served His administration" yet refuse to "submit themselves to God." When leaders "exchange the truth about God for a lie" to serve "worldly philosophies," they invoke God's anger and "disqualify" themselves from "further service in the King's court."

The Danger of Serving Two Masters

"No servant can serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or else he will be loyal to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon" (Luke 16:13). This is not about money. It is about ultimate loyalty. Every Christian in power faces this split at some point. Serve the base? Or serve Christ? The two will eventually conflict.

Leaders who "love the praise of men more than the praise of God" (John 12:43) will inevitably adjust their convictions to maintain their position. It happens gradually. A small compromise. Then another. The frame shifts until you are governing like someone who never heard the gospel. Titus 1:16 provides the diagnostic: "They profess to know God, but in works they deny Him, being abominable, disobedient, and disqualified for every good work." The profession is verbal. The denial is behavioral. Both can coexist in the same person.

The Final Rejection of the Unfaithful Professor

The "apex of human tragedy" is recorded in Matthew 7: "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father in heaven. Many will say to Me in that day, 'Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name, and done many wonders in Your name?' And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you; depart from Me, you who practice lawlessness!'" (Matthew 7:21–23).

Public ministry — even "prophesying" or performing "wonders" — is no proof of genuine conversion if the heart is hostile toward God. The test is not profession. It is fruit. This applies to presidents AND to everyone else. The whitewashed tomb is a human condition, not a political one.

What the Church Should Do — and Not Do

Here is what the church should NOT do: make excuses. "He is under pressure." "It is complicated." "At least he is not as bad as the other side." Jesus did not grade hypocrisy on a curve. Neither should we.

Here is what the church SHOULD do: pray. Not generic "bless the leaders" prayer. Pray for repentance. Pray for conviction. Pray that the fear of God would displace the fear of losing power. God removed Saul. He can restore or remove anyone.

And here is the personal application: "Nevertheless the firm foundation of God stands, having this seal: 'The Lord knows those who are His,' and, 'Everyone who names the name of the Lord is to abstain from wickedness'" (2 Timothy 2:19). The seal has two sides. Side 1: God knows who belongs to Him. Side 2: If you name His name, you abstain from wickedness. Not "navigate it skillfully" or "manage the optics." Abstain. The profession demands the life. Kenya is watching. More importantly, so is God.

The whitewashed tomb is not a political problem. It is a human one. The Lord knows those who are His. And everyone who names His name must abstain from wickedness — not navigate it skillfully, not manage the optics. Abstain.
