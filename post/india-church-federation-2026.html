45 denominations. One federation. 834 reasons why.

Forty-five church leaders across Catholic, Protestant, Evangelical, and Pentecostal traditions formed the National Federation of Churches in India — the first body of its kind in the nation's history. Anti-Christian attacks surged from 151 in 2014 to 834 in 2024. Persecution forced unity that theology could not.

Ecclesiology India Ephesians 4:1-6 Galatians 3:28 Colossians 1:18 Acts 8:1-4 Matthew 16:18 Amos 3:3 Jude 3 2 John 10-11 Revelation 19:7-8 2 Timothy 2:19

Forty-five church leaders walked into a room in Bengaluru last week. Catholic bishops sat beside Pentecostal preachers. Protestant mainline leaders sat beside evangelical pastors. They walked out as the National Federation of Churches in India — the first body of its kind in the nation's history. Cardinal Anthony Poola was named chairperson. The federation spans every major Christian tradition on the subcontinent.[1] The reason they finally sat down together is not a new ecumenical impulse. It is written in a statistic: anti-Christian incidents in India surged from 151 in 2014 to 834 in 2024. More than two attacks per day. The church did not find unity. Persecution forced it.

The world's press will frame this as an administrative story — a coalition formed, a chairman named, a press release issued. The Reformed tradition sees something deeper. It sees a pattern as old as the book of Acts: the church scattered by persecution, and in the scattering, knitted together. It sees the visible church, under fire, remembering who her Head actually is. And it asks the question that every ecumenical moment demands: united around what?

One body — because there is only one Head

I, therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you to walk worthy of the calling with which you were called, with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, endeavoring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

Ephesians 4:1–3

Paul's plea in Ephesians 4:1–6 is not a generic call for religious people to get along. It is grounded in seven ones: one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father. The unity Paul commands is not manufactured by committees. It is a fact of the Spirit's architecture, and the church's task is to maintain what God has already made. The NFCI is an attempt to do that under extreme pressure. The test is whether the unity is anchored to the "one faith" or to a shared sense of threat.

"There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Galatians 3:28). The Reformed tradition has always insisted that Christ alone is the Head of the church (Colossians 1:18). No federation, no cardinal, no denominational structure can share that office. When Indian believers are told they have no right to gather — when anti-conversion laws in Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra make worship into a legal offense — the church's response is not first to organize. It is to remember who authorizes her existence. The state did not grant it. The state cannot revoke it.

The scattering that became a sending

"At that time a great persecution arose against the church which was at Jerusalem; and they were all scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria, except the apostles. Therefore those who were scattered went everywhere preaching the word" (Acts 8:1, 4). This is the grammar of the church under fire. Satan meant to crush the Jesus movement by scattering it. God meant to multiply it. The scattering became a sending. Every home became a church. Every displaced believer became a preacher. The Reformed tradition calls this providence — God "harnesses the deeds of the wicked" to accomplish what no strategic plan could.[2]

India's churches are living this grammar right now. The 834 attacks in 2024 were not random. They were concentrated in states where Christians are a minority and where anti-conversion laws give legal cover to mob violence. The intended effect was fear, silence, retreat. The actual effect was 45 leaders from traditions that have historically regarded each other with suspicion — Catholic and Protestant, mainline and Pentecostal — sitting at the same table and discovering they share the same baptism. Christ's promise holds: "I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it" (Matthew 16:18).

The question every federation must answer

Can two walk together, unless they are agreed?

Amos 3:3

The prophet's question in Amos 3:3 is the single most important test for the NFCI. Unity forged in the fire of persecution is real and precious. Unity that requires doctrinal surrender is neither. The Reformed tradition has been here before. The sixteenth-century Reformers did not separate from Rome because they disliked hierarchy. They separated because the gospel was at stake — justification by faith alone, the final authority of Scripture, the sole headship of Christ. Those were not negotiable then. They are not negotiable now.

This does not mean the NFCI is illegitimate. It means its legitimacy depends on what holds it together. If the federation is a mechanism for shared advocacy — for defending the legal right to worship — it is an expression of common cause among people who share a Lord. If it becomes a mechanism for doctrinal leveling — for treating the differences between Rome and the Reformation as administrative trivia — it has traded truth for solidarity. "Contend earnestly for the faith which was once for all delivered to the saints" (Jude 3). Jude did not write "collaborate earnestly with everyone who uses the name of Christ." He wrote contend. The verb is combative because the stakes are eternal.

The test of Christian unity is never the size of the coalition. It is the clarity of the gospel at its center. A federation formed under persecution is a witness to the world. A federation that cannot say what the gospel is has ceased to be a witness and become a trade association.

"If anyone comes to you and does not bring this doctrine, do not receive him into your house nor greet him; for he who greets him shares in his evil deeds" (2 John 10–11). This is hard-edged, and it is Scripture. The boundary of Christian fellowship is not organizational. It is doctrinal. The church's unity is precious precisely because it is unity in the truth — not unity at the expense of the truth.

The bride will be ready

The final frame is not a federation. It is a wedding. "Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and His wife has made herself ready" (Revelation 19:7–8). Every attempt at visible unity in this age — including the NFCI — is provisional. It anticipates a unity that no committee can produce and no persecution can destroy. On that Day, the church will not need a federation, because she will see her Head face to face.

Until then, Indian Christians will keep gathering. They will keep meeting in homes when church buildings are burned. They will keep baptizing converts when anti-conversion laws threaten prosecution. They will keep forming federations when federation is the only way to be heard. And the Lord who said "I will build My church" will keep His word. The gates of Hades have not prevailed in Jerusalem, in Rome, in Geneva, in Moscow, in Beijing. They will not prevail in Bengaluru. The foundation holds its seal: "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19).
