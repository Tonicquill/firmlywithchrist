The ceasefire says peace. The prophets say wait.

The US-Iran ceasefire holds. Hormuz is under Iranian authority. The peace proposal leaves the nuclear dispute unresolved. Jeremiah called this: healing the wound slightly, saying peace where there is no peace.

Idolatry Iran Jeremiah 6:14 Colossians 1:19-20 Isaiah 48:22 1 Thessalonians 5:3

The ceasefire between the United States and Iran has held since April 8. President Trump predicts a swift end to the war. Pakistan is mediating. Diplomats shuttle between capitals. But Iran has formalized control over the Strait of Hormuz through a new agency, the "Persian Gulf Strait Authority." Saudi Arabia refused to let US forces use its bases. The peace proposal leaves the nuclear program and shipping disputes unresolved. The guns are quiet. The ambitions are not. Jeremiah would know what to call this: "They have healed the hurt of My people slightly, saying, 'Peace, peace!' When there is no peace."[1][2]

Jeremiah 6:14 is the most politically incorrect text in the Bible for a moment like this. The prophet stands in the courts of the temple and indicts the entire religious-political establishment: "They have healed the hurt of My people slightly, saying, 'Peace, peace!' When there is no peace" (Jeremiah 6:14). The Hebrew repeats shalom, shalom — peace, peace — the doubling is incantatory. It is the sound of leaders trying to conjure by repetition what only repentance can produce. The diagnosis in the next verse is that they "were not even ashamed." A ceasefire without confession is not shalom. It is a reload.

The Reformed tradition draws a sharp line between the ceasefire of man and the shalom of God. Ceasefire manages conflict. Shalom reconciles enemies. Ceasefire leaves the weapons intact. Shalom disarms the heart. Ceasefire is negotiated between nations. Shalom is purchased by blood — the blood of the Prince of Peace, who "made peace through the blood of His cross" (Colossians 1:19–20). The current proposal may end a war. It cannot end the rebellion that started it. Isaiah 48:22 gives the verdict: "'There is no peace,' says the LORD, 'for the wicked.'" Not "no ceasefire." No shalom. The two are not the same thing.

For when they say, 'Peace and safety!' then sudden destruction comes upon them.

1 Thessalonians 5:3

Paul warns the Thessalonians that "peace and safety" is the slogan of the deceived: "For when they say, 'Peace and safety!' then sudden destruction comes upon them, as labor pains upon a pregnant woman. And they shall not escape" (1 Thessalonians 5:3). The Greek is eirene kai asphaleia — peace and security. The slogan of the Roman Empire, rebranded for every empire that followed. Paul's point is not that peace is bad. It is that peace declared without reference to the God who gives it is a narcotic. It numbs people to the danger they are still in.

A peace proposal that leaves a nation's nuclear ambitions and shipping chokeholds unresolved is not a failure of diplomacy. It is diplomacy functioning exactly as designed — managing symptoms because it cannot cure the disease. The disease is spiritual rebellion, and no Pakistani-mediated framework addresses it. The Westminster Confession (5.1) teaches that God "sovereignly orders everything He has made." The Strait of Hormuz belongs to Him. The negotiating table was built by Him. The men sitting at it breathe His air. Every document they sign is provisional. Every clause is provisional. Every ceasefire is provisional — until the day when the Prince of Peace returns and every provisional peace is swallowed by the permanent one.

The guns are quiet. The ambitions are not. A ceasefire without confession is not shalom. It is a reload. And every provisional peace expires at the return of the Prince who purchased the permanent one with His blood.
