Heavy Fact-Checking. Heavy Sourcing. Heavy Scripture.

Wild conspiracies. Rigorous truth.

Every conspiracy theory gets the same treatment: verify, source, cross-reference. Then frame it with what Scripture actually says about truth, deception, and the fallen human mind. No echo chambers. No outrage bait. Just evidence and the Word.

Why this category exists

Conspiracy theories are not fringe. In 2026, millions of people — including Christians — believe and share narratives built on pattern-recognition errors, institutional distrust, and algorithmic amplification. These narratives harm real people. They spread faster than corrections. And they thrive in communities that lack a disciplined habit of testing claims.

Christians should be the hardest people to fool. Not because we are smarter. Because Scripture commands discernment — and because we have been told, explicitly, that our hearts lie to us (Jeremiah 17:9), that the first story always sounds right until cross-examined (Proverbs 18:17), and that truth is external — accessible only by testing against evidence and the Word (Acts 17:11).

This category covers conspiracy theories that are trending, viral, or culturally influential. Every piece follows the same protocol: verify each factual claim against primary sources, cross-reference with at least three independent sources, identify what the conspiracy gets wrong and why people believe it anyway, and frame the whole thing theologically — not with vague spiritualism but with specific Scripture.

Our sourcing standard

Every piece in this category meets the following bar:

Primary sources mandatory. Court documents. Official reports. Peer-reviewed studies. Verified footage. Named attribution. No "some say" or "critics claim" without specifying who.

Cross-reference minimum: 3 independent sources per factual claim. If three unrelated, credible sources cannot corroborate a claim, the piece says so.

Link everything. Show your work. Readers should be able to verify every assertion themselves. No appeals to "do your own research" without providing the research.

Distinguish the explained from the unexplained. Some deaths have documented causes. Some disappearances have no known explanation. A faithful witness does not fill gaps with speculation. "We don't know" is an honest answer.

Name the conspiracy theory explicitly. No vague allusions. No "some people are saying." Name the claim. Quote it directly. Link to where it appears.

The biblical frame

Test everything. "Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1).

Hear both sides. "The first one to plead his cause seems right, until his neighbor comes and examines him" (Proverbs 18:17). A conspiracy thread is the first one pleading. The cross-examination is the fact-check. Wisdom waits for the second voice.

Know your own heart. "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked; who can know it?" (Jeremiah 17:9). The conspiracy believer is not uniquely gullible. The skeptic is not uniquely rational. Both operate with the same fallen cognitive equipment.

Judge with righteous judgment. "Do not judge according to appearance, but judge with righteous judgment" (John 7:24). Appearances: similar-sounding names in a thread, a suspicious pattern. Righteous judgment: examine each case individually against the evidence.

The fall damaged our reasoning. "Although they knew God, they did not glorify Him as God, nor were thankful, but became futile in their thoughts, and their foolish hearts were darkened. Professing to be wise, they became fools" (Romans 1:21-22). Discernment is corrective. It pushes against the grain of our own cognition.
