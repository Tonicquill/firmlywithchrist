About

What this is.

Reformed theology for a shaken world. Scripture-grounded. Confessionally rooted. Written from a minority within a minority.

The origin

A few of us with full-time jobs and full lives, who've sat under sound preaching online and wanted a way to share it with friends — without sending ninety-minute sermons or walls of text no one would read. Pinned messages in the group chat got out of hand. So we built this.

We write from a setting where Reformed conviction is a minority within a religious minority — surrounded by versions of Christianity shaped more by prosperity, wellness, and miracle than by sin, sovereignty, and the ordinary means of grace. We also write from a country where public proclamation of Reformed doctrine carries legal and social consequences. The pseudonymity is not a brand choice. It is how this continues to be possible.

What this is

This is what we wish someone had handed us: short, sharp pieces that take the headlines seriously and take Scripture even more seriously. We apply Reformed theology to current events because we believe doctrine is meant to be thought with — not just recited.

We work from the Reformed tradition, specifically:

  • The Westminster Confession of Faith (1647)
  • The Three Forms of Unity (Heidelberg Catechism, Belgic Confession, Canons of Dort)
  • The 1689 London Baptist Confession of Faith

We do not claim to represent all Reformed traditions, nor do we speak for any denomination. We cite the confessions explicitly when grounding a claim.

What it isn't

Not a devotional. Not a church bulletin. Not a news aggregator. We don't chase trends or write for engagement. We're not pastors, theologians, or influencers — just people who think the tradition is worth sharing honestly.

If a piece touches a topic where Reformed Christians disagree (eschatology, theonomy, paedocommunion), we state our position and move on — without pretending the disagreement does not exist.

How it's verified

Every piece passes three gates before publication:

  1. Source verification. The draft is checked against primary sources to confirm confessional citations are accurate and to surface the historic Reformed witness on the topic.
  2. The curious-secularist read. Would a reader who does not share the Reformed frame finish the piece without feeling preached at? The test is not agreement — it is recognition.
  3. The deconstruction-survivor read. Would someone who left an abusive evangelical environment find this disarming or triggering? Confidence and conviction are fine. Pulpit cadence and gatekeeping language are not.

If a piece fails any gate, it goes back to draft. No exceptions. Read the full editorial standards.

Authorial voice

This site is published under a collective pen name. The contributors are confessionally Reformed laypeople who have chosen pseudonymity to keep the focus on the substance rather than the personalities. Pseudonymity is stated openly because transparency about authorship — even limited transparency — is more trustworthy than apparent anonymity.

We do not accept guest posts, sponsored content, or affiliate-driven theology.

Tools and workflow

Research tools assist with source aggregation, draft scaffolding, and editorial workflow. All theological claims, exegetical judgments, and confessional alignment are made by human contributors and verified against primary sources before publication. No tool generates doctrinal positions, interprets scripture, or makes confessional determinations.

If you believe we have misrepresented a text, misstated a fact, or failed our own standards, we read every message. If you are right, we will correct it and log it.
