Genesis 15:6 is six words in Hebrew: w-he-emin b-YHWH wa-yachsheveha lo tzedakah. "And he believed in the LORD, and He counted it to him as righteousness." Six words that Paul will spend an entire chapter — Romans 4 — unpacking. The verse does not say Abram earned righteousness. It does not say Abram's faith was righteousness. It says God counted it to him as righteousness. The verb is chashav — to reckon, to credit, to account. It is a bookkeeping term. God made an entry in Abram's ledger: "Righteousness — credited." Abram did not become righteous by believing. Righteousness was imputed to him, credited to his account, on the basis of faith alone.

Paul's argument in Romans 4 is that this pattern predates the law and operates independently of it. Abraham was justified before circumcision (Genesis 17 comes later), before the law (Exodus 20 comes centuries later), before any works of obedience. "To the one who does not work but believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is counted as righteousness" (Romans 4:5). The phrase "justifies the ungodly" should stop every religious heart cold. God declares the ungodly righteous. Not the reformed. Not the improving. Not the trying-hard. The ungodly. This is the heartbeat of the Reformation. Luther called it "the article by which the church stands or falls." Calvin called imputed righteousness "the principal hinge by which religion is supported."

James 2:23 cites Genesis 15:6 as well, but James is not contradicting Paul. Paul is arguing against works as the ground of justification. James is arguing against a "faith" that has no works as its evidence. Faith alone justifies, but the faith that justifies is never alone. Abraham's faith produced obedience — he left Ur, he waited for Isaac, he offered Isaac on Moriah. But the works were fruit, not root. The order in Genesis is the order in salvation: promise (Genesis 12), faith (Genesis 15:6), covenant sign (Genesis 17), works of obedience (Genesis 22). Grace precedes faith. Faith precedes works. Reversing the order destroys the gospel. Getting the order right is the difference between Christianity and every other religion on earth.
