Before the first day, there was no first day. Before the heavens and the earth, there was no "before." Time itself is a creature. God spoke it into existence along with light, water, stars, and soil. Genesis 1:1 does not begin with an argument for God's existence. It begins with an assertion: "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." The Bible opens with a fact, not a debate. The Hebrew verb bara — "create" — is used exclusively of God in Scripture. No human ever baras anything. We form, shape, arrange, build. Only God brings into being what did not exist. This is the doctrine of creatio ex nihilo — creation out of nothing. God did not work with pre-existing material. He did not organize chaos into cosmos the way a potter works with clay already present. He spoke, and what He spoke became.

The New Testament sharpens the point. John 1:1-3 places the Son at the creation: "All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made." Colossians 1:16 adds that all things were created "through Him and for Him." The universe is not a machine that God wound up and walked away from. It is the personal, purposeful work of a triune God — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — and it exists for Christ. You do not live in neutral space. The world you inhabit was made by the Word, through the Word, and for the Word. Every atom bears His design. Every sunrise announces His lordship.

The opening verse of Scripture is a demolition of every competing worldview in the ancient Near East. No war among the gods. No cosmic battle to establish order. No primordial chaos monster to be subdued. One God. One act. One word. The universe is not divine, not eternal, not independent. It is a creation, and the Creator stands above it, sovereign over every molecule. The Reformers understood this as the foundation of all confidence. God does not merely know the future — He authored the beginning. The One who started everything will finish everything.
