Creation was not finished on the sixth day. It was completed — but not finished. God placed the man in a garden and gave him work: "to tend and keep it" (Genesis 2:15). The original human vocation was cultivation. Before sin entered the world, before thorns and thistles, before cursed ground and sweaty brow, there was work. Adam was a gardener. Eve was a helper. Their labor was not punishment. It was partnership. The Creator who named the stars and separated the seas invited His image-bearers to participate in the ongoing act of ordering creation.

The naming of the animals in Genesis 2:19-20 is the first recorded human act of dominion. God brought the creatures to Adam "to see what he would call them." This is astonishing. The omniscient Creator, who already knows every star by name (Psalm 147:4), waits to see what Adam will say. He gives His creature genuine agency within the bounds of His sovereignty. Human naming is a derivative act of authority — the kind of dominion that flows from relationship with God, not autonomy from Him. Adam is not a dictator over creation. He is a steward under the ultimate Owner.

This is the doctrine of vocation. Every lawful human calling — farmer, coder, teacher, mother, waste collector, executive, artist — is an extension of Adam's original mandate. Your work matters. It is not a distraction from "spiritual" things. It is spiritual. "Whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God" (1 Corinthians 10:31). The Reformers recovered this truth from the medieval dichotomy that made priests "spiritual" and everyone else second-class. All of life is coram Deo — before the face of God. The carpenter's nail, the doctor's diagnosis, the teacher's lesson plan, the parent's midnight feeding — all of it is kingdom work when done in faith. We are not waiting to escape this world. We are stewarding it until the King returns.
