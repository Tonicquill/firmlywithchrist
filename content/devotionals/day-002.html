Genesis 1:26 drops into the creation account like a thunderclap. God has been speaking things into existence — light, sky, land, plants, sun, moon, sea creatures, birds, livestock — and each act of creation is called "good." Then the rhythm breaks. God pauses. The language shifts from "Let there be" to "Let Us make." The triune God takes counsel within Himself and declares His intention to create something unlike everything else: a being in His own image and likeness.

The imago Dei — the image of God — is not a quality we possess alongside other qualities. It is not intelligence, or creativity, or moral capacity, though those may reflect it. The image of God is the fundamental fact of what a human being is. Every man, woman, and child bears it equally. It is not earned, not graduated, not lost at the Fall — defaced but not erased. This is why murder is wrong (Genesis 9:6). This is why gossip is evil (James 3:9). This is why every human encounter is an encounter with something sacred. The person in front of you does not merely have dignity — they are dignity, stamped with the likeness of their Maker.

The Westminster Shorter Catechism opens with the question: "What is the chief end of man?" Answer: "Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy Him forever." The image is not just a status — it is a purpose. You were made to reflect. A mirror does not create light; it catches light and sends it outward. Human beings were made to catch the glory of God and reflect it back in worship, in work, in love, in dominion over creation. That is why sin is such a catastrophe. It is not just rule-breaking. It is image-shattering. The mirror turned away from the light and toward itself. And that is why redemption is restoration: Christ, "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15), comes to restore what was broken.
