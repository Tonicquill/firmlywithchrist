Genesis 50:20 is the theological thesis statement of the entire Joseph narrative: "As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good." The Hebrew verb chashav — "meant" — is the same word used in Genesis 15:6 for how God "counted" righteousness to Abraham. Two actors. Two meanings. One verb. The brothers intended evil, and they are held fully responsible for it: they sold their brother into slavery, deceived their father, and lived with guilt for decades. God intended good through the same events: Joseph was positioned to save Egypt, the surrounding nations, and the covenant line from starvation. Both meanings are real. Both agents are active. And God's meaning is sovereign over man's meaning.

This is the biblical doctrine of concurrence — God's governance of all events operates simultaneously with genuine human moral agency. The Westminster Confession (5.1-4) affirms that God "sovereignly orders everything He has made" and that His providence extends "even to the first fall, and all other sins of angels and men." But it adds carefully: "and that not by a bare permission, but such as has joined with it a most wise and powerful bounding, and otherwise ordering and governing of them, in a manifold dispensation, to His own holy ends." God does not commit evil. God governs evil toward good ends. The brothers meant it for evil. God meant it for good. Both statements are true. Neither is diminished. The sovereignty of God is the comfort of the afflicted. Your suffering is not meaningless. It is not random. It is under the governance of a God who weaves evil intentions into good outcomes without ever becoming the author of the evil.

Romans 8:28 is the New Testament echo of Genesis 50:20: "And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to His purpose." Not all things are good. Joseph's enslavement was not good. His imprisonment was not good. But God works all things — including the evil things — toward a good end. The cross is the ultimate demonstration: the worst act of human evil in history, the murder of the Son of God, was also the predetermined plan of God for the salvation of the world (Acts 2:23). God does not merely tolerate evil. He governs it. He does not merely permit suffering. He redeems it. And the good toward which all things are working is not merely relief from suffering. It is conformity to the image of His Son (Romans 8:29).
