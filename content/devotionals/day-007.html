By Genesis 6, God sees that "the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (Genesis 6:5). This is the most comprehensive statement of human depravity in the Old Testament. Not some thoughts. Not occasional inclinations. "Every intention" — "only evil" — "continually." Three intensifiers stacked to eliminate any loophole. The Lord "regretted that He had made man on the earth, and it grieved Him to His heart" (6:6). This is the language of divine sorrow, not divine surprise. God does not learn new information. The grief is real, but it is the grief of a Father who always knew, not of a Creator caught off guard.

Amid the universal corruption, one man stands out: "Noah found favor in the eyes of the Lord" (6:8). Noah was not sinless. He was a sinner who believed God. Hebrews 11:7 says, "By faith Noah, being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark for the saving of his household." Noah built a boat on dry land because God told him rain was coming, even though rain had never fallen before. Every day of construction was a sermon. Every plank was a warning. The world carried on — eating, drinking, marrying — until the day Noah entered the ark and the door shut (Matthew 24:38-39).

The flood is a difficult story. It involves universal judgment. It involves the death of every land creature outside the ark. The modern reader flinches. But the flood is also a story of salvation. The ark is not just a boat — it is a type of Christ. The waters of judgment fell on the ark so that those inside would be saved. The same waters that destroyed the world lifted the ark above destruction. Baptism, Peter says, "corresponds to this, not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience through the resurrection of Jesus Christ" (1 Peter 3:21). The flood preaches the gospel: judgment is real, escape is narrow, the door is one, and God Himself provides the vessel of salvation.
