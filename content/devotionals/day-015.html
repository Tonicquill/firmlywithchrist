Genesis 18 contains the most audacious prayer in the Old Testament. Abraham, the friend of God, stands before the LORD and negotiates. "Will You sweep away the righteous with the wicked? Far be it from You! Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is just?" God agrees: if fifty righteous are found, the city is spared. Abraham pushes: forty-five? Forty? Thirty? Twenty? Ten? Each time God agrees. Abraham stops at ten. He should have kept going. Not because God would have changed His mind — but because Abraham's question was better than his math. The Judge of all the earth always does what is just. And in Sodom, the evidence was overwhelming: the city could not produce ten righteous people.

The destruction of Sodom in Genesis 19 is difficult to read. Fire and sulfur rain from heaven. The cities of the plain are obliterated. Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt — not out of curiosity, but out of longing. Jesus uses her as a warning: "Remember Lot's wife" (Luke 17:32-33). Her backward glance was a divided heart. She was physically rescued from Sodom, but Sodom was not rescued from her heart. Judgment reveals where your treasure is. Lot's wife treasured what was being destroyed, and she became what she treasured. The warning echoes through the centuries: do not love what God is judging.

2 Peter 2:6-7 draws a theological conclusion from the ashes of Sodom: God "condemned them to extinction, making them an example of what is going to happen to the ungodly" — and He "rescued righteous Lot." The pattern holds: God judges sin and rescues His own. Every judgment in Scripture is both a verdict and a preview. The flood, Sodom, the exile — each is a smaller judgment pointing toward the final one. And each smaller judgment contains a rescue pointing toward the final rescue. The Judge of all the earth does what is just. And the Judge of all the earth has provided a way of escape. The ark was wood and pitch. The rescue now is blood and righteousness — Christ's blood, Christ's righteousness, credited to everyone who, like Abraham, believes God.
