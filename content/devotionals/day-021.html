Jacob is heading home after twenty years in Haran. He left as a fugitive. He returns as a wealthy man — two wives, eleven children, massive flocks. But Esau awaits, and Jacob is terrified. He divides his camp, sends elaborate gifts, and spends the night alone on the banks of the Jabbok River while his family crosses ahead. Everything he has built in twenty years is on the other side of the river. He is alone. Then a man wrestles with him until daybreak. The man is no ordinary man, and the wrestling match is no ordinary combat. Hosea 12:4 identifies the opponent as an angel. Jacob identifies Him as God: "I have seen God face to face, and yet my life has been delivered" (Genesis 32:30).

Jacob refuses to let go until he receives a blessing. And the blessing comes through an injury: the man touches Jacob's hip socket and puts it out of joint. Jacob wrestles the rest of the night with a dislocated hip. The man gives him a new name: Israel, "he strives with God." The name marks both the struggle and the victory. Jacob had spent his life striving — grabbing Esau's heel, stealing Esau's blessing, wrangling with Laban over wages. Every striving was an attempt to secure by his own cunning what God had already promised to give by grace. At Peniel, God wrestles the striving out of Jacob — literally. The limp is the blessing. Jacob limps into the sunrise a broken man and a new man, a cripple and a prince.

The pattern recurs throughout Scripture. Paul's thorn in the flesh is a Peniel moment: "When I am weak, then I am strong" (2 Corinthians 12:10). God blesses through breaking, strengthens through weakening. The limp is not a punishment for striving. It is the cure for it. You cannot wrestle God and walk away without a limp. But you also cannot wrestle God and walk away without a blessing. The two go together. The limp is the proof that you met God and survived. Israel walked with a limp for the rest of his life, and every step reminded him: the blessing comes through the breaking, and the God who breaks you is the God who blesses you.
