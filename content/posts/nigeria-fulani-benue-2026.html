On the evening of May 26, Muslim Fulani militia attacked three locations across Benue State, central Nigeria, and killed four Christians. In Ukpamaju village, Otukpo County, farmer Friday Omoha and tractor operator Francis Liambe were gunned down by roughly fifteen armed men chanting Allahu Akbar and wielding AK-47 rifles. Hours later, on the Otukpo–Enugu highway in Ohimini County, militants attacked a commercial bus and killed a security operative who tried to protect the Christian passengers. In Ojantele community, Apa County, twenty-five-year-old Ngbede Ocholongwa was killed. Otukpo County Chairman Maxwell Ogiri confirmed the incidents. Security analyst Pablo William called it a “sustained pattern of organized rural Islamic terror targeting vulnerable Christian farming communities.” Survivor Stephen Omoha hid in nearby bushes while the gunfire rang out and the militants spoke Fulfulde and Hausa. Activist Meddy Olotu said communities are living in constant fear. Resident John Ehoda said people believe the authorities know where the militia camps are but do nothing to dismantle them. The Nigerian federal government has already been accused by the U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom of “tolerating” religious violence and failing to prosecute killers. Over 1.3 million people have been displaced in the Middle Belt. Four Christians dead in one evening. And the authorities who should protect them have “neglected the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faith.”[1]

Jesus warned His disciples: "'Are not two sparrows sold for a copper coin? And not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Do not fear therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows'" (Matthew 10:29-31). The Fulani militant with the AK-47 does not know that Friday Omoha's hairs were numbered. He does not know that Francis Liambe was of more value than many sparrows. He does not know that Ngbede Ocholongwa fell to the ground apart from his Father's will — which means the Father saw it, the Father allowed it, and the Father has already numbered the days of the militant who pulled the trigger. The sparrow that falls is not an accident of rural violence. It is an appointment with the One who counts the hairs on the heads of the beheaded.
