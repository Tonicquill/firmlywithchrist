The Vatican issued a final warning to the Society of St. Pius X on Tuesday: consecrate four new bishops without papal consent on July 1, and you will have committed a schismatic act incurring automatic excommunication. Cardinal Victor Manuel Fernandez delivered the ultimatum in writing. Pope Leo XIV is reportedly "praying for enlightenment" for the SSPX leadership. The Catholic press is framing this as the gravest challenge to Leo XIV's authority since his election.[1] Two sides. One dispute. One question that neither side is asking: who said the pope was the Head of the church in the first place?

To the secular press, this is an institutional power struggle — a breakaway traditionalist group defying the Vatican's chain of command. To the Catholic world, it is a crisis of ecclesial authority. To the Reformed Christian watching from outside both camps, it is something more fundamental: a live demonstration of what happens when a human office claims a throne that belongs exclusively to Jesus Christ. The SSPX and the Vatican are fighting over who holds the keys. Scripture already settled that question. The keys belong to the One who died and rose again.

One Head. Not two. Not a successor.

And He is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything He might be preeminent.

Colossians 1:18

Paul's declaration in Colossians 1:18 is not ambiguous. Christ is the head of the body, the church. Not was. Not will be again when the current crisis resolves. Is. Present tense. Ongoing. Exclusive. The Head has not vacated His office. He has not delegated His authority to a successor. He has not shared His throne with a vicar. The church has one Head because the body only has one source of life. A body with two heads is a monstrosity. A church with two heads is a contradiction.

"He put all things under His feet and gave Him as head over all things to the church, which is His body, the fullness of Him who fills all in all" (Ephesians 1:22–23). The Father gave Christ to the church as Head. The Father did not give the church permission to appoint an understudy. The Reformed tradition confesses this in Westminster Confession of Faith 25.1: Christ is the "only Head" of the church. Not the ultimate Head. Not the heavenly Head while the pope is the earthly Head. The only Head. The word is a wall. Any human being who claims that office stands on the wrong side of it.

The fight that reveals the flaw

Observe what this dispute actually exposes. The SSPX claims it must consecrate bishops to preserve Catholic tradition against a modernizing Rome. The Vatican claims the SSPX must submit to papal authority or sever itself from the true church. Both arguments assume the same premise: that the Roman communion is the church Christ founded, and that its visible head possesses the authority to bind and loose. The fight is not over whether the pope is the Head. The fight is over whether this particular pope is exercising his headship faithfully. The premise goes unexamined.

But Christ examined it. "You are not to be called rabbi, for you have one teacher, and you are all brothers. And call no man your father on earth, for you have one Father, who is in heaven. Neither be called instructors, for you have one instructor, the Christ" (Matthew 23:8–10). The Roman system calls its chief officer "Holy Father." Christ explicitly forbade calling any man on earth "father" in the spiritual sense. The SSPX does not object to this title. It only objects to how this particular Holy Father is using it. The Reformed tradition objects to the title itself, because Christ already did.

What schism actually is

Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?

1 Corinthians 1:13

Paul's anguished questions to the Corinthian church cut to the heart of what schism actually is. The Corinthians were dividing into factions — "I follow Paul," "I follow Apollos," "I follow Cephas." They were not denying Christ. They were attaching His name to rival camps. Paul's response is not to adjudicate which faction has the stronger claim to legitimacy. It is to demolish the premise of factionalism itself. Is Christ divided? Was anyone other than Christ crucified for you? Were you baptized into any name other than the Triune name?

Schism, in Scripture's definition, is not departure from a particular institutional structure. It is the division of Christ's body into competing parties that treat human leaders as heads. By that definition, the Roman system was schismatic long before the SSPX existed — because it divided the visible church by elevating a single bishop to an office Scripture never established. The SSPX is not the solution to that schism. It is a schism within the schism. A faction breaking from a faction. Neither side can cry "schism" against the other without indicting the whole structure that made the rupture inevitable.

The Roman Catholic Church claims the pope is the visible head of the church on earth. The SSPX claims this particular pope is a bad one. The Reformed faith answers both with Colossians 1:18: there is no visible head of the church on earth, because the actual Head is not absent. He reigns. He needs no vicar.

The Head will not be usurped

The final frame on this dispute is not an ecclesiology textbook. It is a coronation scene. "Then the seventh angel sounded: And there were loud voices in heaven, saying, 'The kingdoms of this world have become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ, and He shall reign forever and ever'" (Revelation 11:15). Every office that claims headship over Christ's church — the papacy, the SSPX's alternative structure, any human institution that places itself between the Head and His body — will be swept away on that Day. Not reformed. Not restructured. Consumed.

This side of glory, the church will continue to fracture. Rome will issue ultimatums. Breakaway groups will consecrate bishops. Conferences of Catholic bishops will debate whether to recognize the new bishops' ordinations. The Reformed Christian watches none of this as a neutral observer. We watch it as people who confessed at the Reformation that the papacy is not the church, because the church's Head never delegated His office. That confession was not a sixteenth-century historical accident. It was a biblical recovery. The Head is Christ. The Head remains Christ. The foundation holds its seal: "The Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19). That seal is not stamped on Vatican letterhead. It is inscribed on every heart that belongs to the only Head who ever died for His body and rose again.
