Mosab Hassan Yousef was born into the house of his enemies. His father, Sheikh Hassan Yousef, is a founding leader of Hamas — the Islamist militant organization that has waged war against Israel for decades and explicitly calls for the destruction of the Jewish state. The son was raised in the ideology. He breathed it. As a young man he was imprisoned by Israel and witnessed Hamas members torture fellow Palestinians suspected of collaboration. What he saw broke the spell. He walked out of prison, walked into a Shin Bet intelligence office, and became "The Green Prince" — the most valuable Israeli asset inside Hamas. For a decade he fed information that saved countless lives. Then he became a Christian.[1][2]

The world has a category for this man: defector, traitor, spy, whistleblower. Scripture has older and more precise categories. Saul of Tarsus was a Pharisee who breathed threats and murder against the church until Christ struck him blind on the Damascus road and turned the chief persecutor into the chief apostle. "John 1:12–13" is the theological operating system: you are born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. Mosab's biological bloodline ran straight into the Hamas leadership. His new birth runs somewhere else entirely. The Reformed tradition, reflecting on texts like Matthew 10:37, teaches that "the Lord knows those who are His" (2 Timothy 2:19). The seal is not ancestral lineage. It is divine election. A man born among the King's enemies became a son of the King.

The Green Prince: What a spy's life reveals about Providence

But as for you, you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good, in order to bring it about as it is this day, to save many people alive.

Genesis 50:20

The Shin Bet gave him the codename "The Green Prince" — green for Hamas, prince because his father was Hamas royalty. For ten years Mosab fed Israeli intelligence information that prevented suicide bombings, exposed terror cells, and saved Israeli and Palestinian lives. The calculus is stark. A man raised to destroy Israel became the man who preserved it — from inside his father's own organization. The pattern is Joseph. Sold into Egypt by his own brothers, Joseph rose to preserve the very people who betrayed him. "Genesis 50:20" does not describe a comfortable providence. It describes a violent one — where God harnesses the deeds of the wicked, including the terrorist infrastructure of Hamas, to accomplish His good and perfect ends. The Westminster Confession of Faith (5.1) teaches that God "sovereignly orders everything He has made." Everything. The Hamas leadership. The prison cell where Mosab first witnessed Hamas brutality. The Shin Bet recruitment. No link in the chain was outside divine jurisdiction.

Mosab's decade-long intelligence work was not merely a geopolitical operation. It was a theological statement. Every suicide bombing thwarted was a life spared by the hidden hand of the God who "kills and makes alive" (1 Samuel 2:6). The insider who saves lives from within the enemy camp is a living parable of grace — the ultimate insider being Christ Himself, who entered enemy territory in human flesh to rescue His own. "For if when we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life" (Romans 5:10).

The word of their testimony

In 2008 Mosab went public. He had already been reading the Bible in secret. The Jesus he encountered in the Gospels did not match the prophet Isa he had been taught about in Islam. One was a teacher. The other was the Son of God who died for sins and rose again. The difference is infinite. Mosab chose the Jesus of Scripture and publicly identified as a Christian — a decision that made him an apostate under Islamic law, a traitor to his family's legacy, and a man permanently severed from the community he was born into. He did it anyway. "Revelation 12:11" describes exactly this posture: they overcame by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony, and they loved not their lives unto death.

Mosab has continued speaking publicly into 2026 — drawing sold-out crowds at American universities, challenging former President Trump's Iran posture ("what makes you believe you have what it takes to defeat the Islamic Republic?"), calling for a peaceful Israeli counter-flotilla to break the Gaza blockade narrative, sitting for hours-long interviews with secular outlets like the Ayn Rand Institute where he lays out the ideological architecture of Hamas's death cult from the inside.[3][4] He is not a seminary graduate. He does not speak in evangelical clichés. He speaks like a man who walked out of the darkness and knows exactly what the darkness costs.

Some Christians are uncomfortable with him. He does not fit the mold. His politics are complicated. His theology is still forming. But the Reformed tradition has always taught that God "has chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise, and God has chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the things which are mighty" (1 Corinthians 1:27). The son of a Hamas founder who becomes a Shin Bet spy and then a Christian is not a tidy testimony. It is a biblical one. The Bible is full of figures whose conversion arcs are messy, dangerous, and politically radioactive. Saul. Rahab. The demoniac of Gerasene. None of them fit the mold. All of them testified anyway.

A man born in the house of God's enemies became a son of God, a spy for life, and a public witness for Christ. The bloodline could not save him. The blood of the Lamb could. He loved not his life unto death — and lives to tell of it.
